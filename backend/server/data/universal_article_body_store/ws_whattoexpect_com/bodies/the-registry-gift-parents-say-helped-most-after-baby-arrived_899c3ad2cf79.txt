When you’re building a baby registry, most of your energy goes into the gear. The stroller you spent weeks researching. The bassinet everyone swears by. The bottles that promise less gas. Those things absolutely matter. But after baby arrives, you quickly realize the hardest part of the newborn stage isn’t choosing the right products. It’s managing everyday life while running on almost no sleep.

One thing that comes up again and again in the What to Expect community? Food. Between feedings, pediatrician visits, and long nights, something as simple as figuring out dinner can suddenly feel overwhelming. That’s why many families recommend adding some kind of meal support to your registry, whether that’s a meal train, restaurant gift cards, or a food delivery credit.

Food delivery services like DoorDash pop up often in community threads. Moms talk about using it to order dinner after a long day, grab lunch during a contact nap or send a quick grocery order when the fridge is empty. In those early weeks, it’s the kind of convenience that ends up making a bigger difference than you expect.

Helpful During Postpartum Recovery

For many, the ability to order food instead of cooking is especially helpful while recovering physically after birth. Inormally love cooking and make most of our meals at home, but after my C-section, even standing at the stove felt like too much. We ended up relying on DoorDash far more than I expected, ordering both takeout and groceries during those first few weeks when cooking just wasn’t happening.

Support During Difficult Moments

Food delivery can also be helpful during more unexpected situations. A friend of mine experienced this when her baby spent a month in the NICU. Our friend group pooled together and sent her and her husband a DoorDash gift card so they could easily order meals to the hotel they were staying in near the hospital. It was a small gesture, but it made a stressful and exhausting time a little easier to navigate.

Why Many Add It to Their Registry

Friends and family naturally gravitate toward buying baby gear, but once the baby arrives, what often helps most is support with everyday life. Adding something like a DoorDash gift card to your registry gives people an easy way to help when the newborn haze hits. And unlike most baby items, it doesn’t take up any space in your house.

It Comes in Handy During Pregnancy, Too

Food delivery doesn’t just come up after baby arrives. It’s also a common theme in pregnancy threads, especially during the first trimester when nausea and food aversions can make cooking feel impossible, something I experienced myself when even thinking about making dinner felt like too much.

In one recent What to Expect community discussion , a mom at nine weeks pregnant wrote that she and her husband had been ordering DoorDash almost every day because “everything in our fridge makes me want to gag.” Others quickly chimed in saying the same thing: leftovers suddenly looked disgusting, cravings changed daily and sometimes the only thing that sounded edible was something specific they could quickly order. (If you have a pregnant friend who is currently going through it, consider sending a little DoorDash credit. It might end up being exactly what she needs.)

Other Food Support Parents Appreciate

Some families mix in restaurant-specific gift cards depending on what’s available locally, and many organize meal trains, or coordinate takeout drop-offs with friends. But across the What to Expect community, the takeaway is the same: in those early newborn weeks, and sometimes even earlier in pregnancy, having an easy way to get a meal on the table can be surprisingly helpful.

After covering baby gear for years and building my own registries, I’ve learned that the most useful gifts aren’t always the big-ticket ones. Sometimes they’re the small things that make day-to-day life easier while you adjust to life with a newborn.
