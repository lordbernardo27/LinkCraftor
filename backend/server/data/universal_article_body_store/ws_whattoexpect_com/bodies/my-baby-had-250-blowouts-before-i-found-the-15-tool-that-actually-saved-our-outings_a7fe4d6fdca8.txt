By month three, my baby was having two to three blowouts a day. We aren't talking about occasional leaks; we’re talking full-on, outfit-decimating, "burn-everything" blowouts. Once she started sleeping longer stretches, the overnight situations became even more daunting. More time meant more volume and more damage. This went on for months. According to my calculations, that’s easily 250+ blowouts between months three and six.

A typical day meant carrying extra outfits everywhere, doubling up on diapers, and using far too many wipes just to stay afloat. Leaving the house felt like a tactical mission, and public diaper changes were even worse. Trying to manage a blowout in a cramped bathroom while keeping everything contained? I simply wasn't equipped for it. The breaking point came during a week-long trip to Utah when she was six months old. She blew out in the Pack 'n Play… Every. Single. Night. These were high-caliber messes that soaked through the diaper, pajamas, and sleep sack, straight into the mattress. We were washing bedding daily, and I was at my wits' end.

I tried switching diaper brands, sizing up, and every "hack" the internet recommended. Nothing mattered. The issue wasn’t the diaper; it was the sheer volume and consistency. I realized blowouts were inevitable—what I actually needed was a way to make the aftermath less chaotic. That’s when I discovered the highly reviewed Peekapoo Mini Disposable Changing Pads , and started putting them everywhere: under her crib sheet, layered in the car seat, packed in every diaper bag, stashed around the house. They didn’t stop the blowouts (nothing could), but they contained them. Instead of poop ruining a car seat or a hotel mattress, it stayed on the pad. I could simply fold up the mess, toss it, and move on. While the outfit was usually a total loss, everything else was saved. If you’ve ever had to deep-clean a car seat, you know that is no small feat.
