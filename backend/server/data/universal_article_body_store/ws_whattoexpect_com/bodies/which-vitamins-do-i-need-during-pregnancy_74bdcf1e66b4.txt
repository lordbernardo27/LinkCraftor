Now that you're pregnant, it's more important than ever for your daily diet to include key nutrients. From calcium to zinc, here's what you need and why.

Most nutrition requirements during pregnancy are the same as they were pre-pregnancy, with a few minor tweaks. Getting your daily dose of essential vitamins and minerals is one of the best things you can do to support your growing baby and lower your risk of pregnancy complications including anemia , gestational diabetes , and preeclampsia .

Here's your guide to all the essential nutrients you'll need during pregnancy and beyond — and the best foods to put on your plate.

Key Takeaways

Most nutrient needs stay the same during pregnancy, but a few become especially important. Key nutrients like folic acid, iron, calcium, choline, and DHA play critical roles in your baby's growth and development while supporting your own health.

A balanced diet can help lower the risk of pregnancy complications. Getting enough essential vitamins, minerals, healthy fats, and fiber may reduce the risk of conditions such as anemia, gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, and low birth weight.

Food should be your first source of nutrition whenever possible. Try to fill your plate with nutrient-rich whole foods — including fruits, vegetables, whole grains, dairy, seafood, lean proteins, legumes, and nuts.

Calcium

Calcium is the most abundant mineral in the body, with 99% stored in the bones and teeth.

Why do I need calcium during pregnancy?

Your growing baby gets all the calcium she needs from your body, mainly for her developing bones and teeth . That means if you're not getting the right amounts in your diet, your body will leach calcium from your own bones, making you more susceptible to osteoporosis. But don't worry; it's easy to work calcium into your diet .

How much calcium do I need during pregnancy?

Pregnant women 19 and older need 1,000 milligrams (mg) of calcium per day, and pregnant women 18 and under need 1,300 mg.

Choline

Choline is a vitamin-like essential nutrient that's involved in many physiological processes, including the production of neurotransmitters (i.e., chemical messengers) in the brain. It's also needed to make phospholipids, which help with the structure of cell membranes.

Why do I need choline during pregnancy?

Choline is vital for your baby's brain development — specifically the memory center of the brain. It can also affect cognition later in life — it's good for the aging brain, too! Some studies have shown adequate choline intake during pregnancy decreases the risk of neural tube defects.

How much choline do I need during pregnancy?

When you're pregnant, you need 450 mg of choline per day.

Copper

Copper is an essential trace mineral and micronutrient necessary for the proper growth, development, and maintenance of bone, brain, heart, and organ tissue.

Why do I need copper during pregnancy?

Copper is especially important during pregnancy, when your blood supply doubles and your energy levels drop. This trace element also helps create your baby's heart, blood vessels, and cells, as well as nervous, immune, and skeletal systems.

How much copper do I need during pregnancy?

You need 1,000 micrograms (mcg) of copper per day during pregnancy.

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)

DHA is one of the omega-3 fatty acids found throughout the body, mainly in the brain and eyes.

Why do I need DHA during pregnancy?

DHA is a key omega-3 fatty acid for visual and mental development of the fetus and newborn infant. Around week 8, your baby's eye structure is becoming more complex, and the retina — the layer of tissue at the back of the eye that perceives and processes light — is beginning to form, making it an excellent time to bulk up on DHA.

How much DHA do I need during pregnancy?

You need 200 to 300 mg of DHA per day during pregnancy.

Fiber

Your body doesn't digest this type of carbohydrate, but it's essential because it helps food move efficiently through your system, delivering other nutrients to the organs that need them.

Why do I need fiber during pregnancy?

Fiber-rich foods are especially helpful during pregnancy, as they work to regulate blood sugar levels and prevent gestational diabetes. Plus, fiber can lower the risk of high blood pressure and may help protect against preeclampsia. An added bonus of continuing to get enough fiber throughout pregnancy? It may help with an unsettled tummy and does wonders for the constipation that often plagues expectant moms.

How much fiber do I need during pregnancy?

Pregnant women need 25 to 35 grams (g) of fiber per day.

Folic acid (vitamin B9)

Folic acid isn't just important when you're trying to conceive or early on in your pregnancy. You'll want to fill up on it throughout these nine months, since it plays an important role in the production of red blood cells and helps your baby's brain and spinal cord develop properly.

Folic acid helps the body break down, use, and create protein, the building block of our cells. It also plays a role in DNA creation and assists in the formation of your baby's heart and circulatory system. Early in pregnancy, it can reduce the risk of neural tube defects like spina bifida.

Why do I need folic acid during pregnancy?

Because most birth defects develop in the first few weeks of pregnancy, it's important to load up as early as possible. But you need to keep up your folic acid intake as your baby grows, too, since it aids in the development of key systems in your baby's body and lowers the risk of preterm birth , gestational diabetes, and heart defects.

How much folic acid do I need during pregnancy?

You need 400 mcg of folic acid per day starting at least one month before conception and 400 to 600 mcg throughout pregnancy and while breastfeeding .

Iodine

Iodine is a trace element that's found in food. It plays a big role in brain development and the creation of thyroid hormones.

Why do I need iodine during pregnancy?

Iodine is one of the most important minerals for baby's brain and nervous system development. It also helps maintain normal function of the thyroid, a gland in the base of the neck that regulates hormones that control core body functions, including metabolism, heart rate, and body temperature.

Iodine deficiency during pregnancy is the most preventable cause of intellectual disability in the world, according to the World Health Organization (WHO) , but most Americans get enough in their everyday diets.

How much iodine do I need during pregnancy?

Pregnant women need 220 to 250 mcg of iodine per day and no more than 1,100 mcg per day.

Iron

Iron is an important mineral that's part of the protein hemoglobin, which is found in red blood cells and is tasked with transferring oxygen from the lungs to the tissues and returning carbon dioxide from the tissues back to the lungs. Iron also plays a role in growth, development, cell functioning, and building hormones and connective tissue.

Why do I need iron during pregnancy?

It's normal to be tired (or downright exhausted) during pregnancy, which may be in part due to your body's increased demand for iron. The mineral also plays a key role by helping carry oxygen through the body for both you and baby.

How much iron do I need during pregnancy?

You need at least 27 mg of iron per day, but check with your health care practitioner. Depending on your iron levels, you may need additional iron.

Magnesium

Magnesium is an essential mineral that helps regulate almost every system in the body. It works in combination with calcium to relax and contract muscles.

Why do I need magnesium during pregnancy?

In addition to strengthening baby's bones and teeth, magnesium works to stimulate enzyme function, regulate insulin, and control blood sugar levels. If you're deficient in magnesium, you may feel fatigued and lack muscle strength (as if you needed any more of that these days). Other severe consequences can include poor fetal growth or preeclampsia.

How much magnesium do I need during pregnancy?

During pregnancy, you need 350 to 360 mg of magnesium per day if you're 19 or older and 400 mg if you're 18 or younger.

Your body is working hard to create bone and cartilage for your growing baby, which is made possible in part by manganese. It also plays a role in metabolizing carbohydrates, amino acids, and cholesterol that your baby needs to grow, and helps protect those brand-new cells from damage.

Omega-3 fatty acids

These healthy fats fight inflammation, reduce blood pressure, and increase the good kind of cholesterol (HDL) while lowering the bad (LDL).

Why do I need omega-3 fatty acids during pregnancy?

Omega-3s help develop and sustain the health of your baby's growing heart, immune system, brain, and eyes, especially during the third trimester when your baby's brain growth is at its peak. Research has even found that getting enough omega-3s during pregnancy, the most notable being DHA, may help fight postpartum depression , regulate pregnancy-related mood swings , and reduce the risk of preterm birth and very low birth weight .

How much omega-3 fatty acids do I need during pregnancy?

Pregnant women need 650 mg of omega-3 fatty acids per day (including 300 mg of DHA).

Omega-6 fatty acids

While omega-6 fatty acids are essential for human health, your body cannot create them on its own, meaning your only means of getting them is through food. Luckily, eating a typical diet will easily cover your needs.

Why do I need omega-6 fatty acids during pregnancy?

Along with omega-3 fatty acids, omega-6 fatty acids play an important role in your baby's brain function, as well as physical growth and development. As they're a particular type of polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA), omega-6s also are important for skin, hair, and bone growth, as well as for maintaining proper functioning of the reproductive system and the body's metabolism levels.

Phosphorus

After calcium, phosphorus is the second most abundant mineral in your body, and 85% of it is found in your bones and teeth.

Why do I need phosphorus during pregnancy?

Phosphorus is very important for most bodily functions, namely muscle movement, blood clotting, and kidney and nerve function, as well as tissue and cell repair. Getting your fill during pregnancy means your baby is getting hers, too.

How much phosphorus do I need during pregnancy?

Potassium

This mineral is found in many delicious food sources and is one of seven essential macrominerals, along with calcium, magnesium, phosphorus, sodium, chloride, and sulfur. It's tasked with maintaining proper organ function.

Why do I need potassium during pregnancy?

Now that your body is working for two, your fluid levels increase significantly. Potassium works with sodium to maintain proper fluid balance in cells. This mineral also regulates blood pressure (important, since high blood pressure is a characteristic of preeclampsia) and is known to help your body maintain muscle (meaning fewer aches, pains, and cramping!).

How much potassium do I need during pregnancy?

Protein

Protein is the basic material of all living cells. It's made up of amino acids and is a key component in all the tissues of the body, helping to repair and build muscle, bone, skin, hair, nails, and blood. It's also involved in creating and regulating hormones, enzymes, and chemical reactions within our bodies.

Why do I need protein during pregnancy?

Protein and its amino acids are the building blocks of human cells, responsible for constructing your baby's beautiful face and cute little body. Towards the middle to end of your pregnancy, protein is even more important as your baby's organs begin to transform.

(You'll also want to make sure that you're continuing to get your daily dose of vitamin B6, which helps break down protein.)

Sodium

Sodium is one of two minerals found in table salt that works to regulate the body's fluid, temperature, and pH levels. Without enough sodium, your muscles, nerves, and organs wouldn't function properly — but most Americans consume more than they need.

Because your body's fluid levels increase when you're expecting, sodium comes in handy to keep it all in balance.

Vitamin A

Vitamin A is a group of fat-soluble compounds and antioxidants — including retinol, retinal, and retinoic acid — that support immune function, vision, reproduction, and healthy cell growth.

There are two forms of vitamin A in the diet: provitamin A carotenoids and preformed vitamin A (retinol). Preformed vitamin A is found in animal products such as dairy, meat, and fish.

Provitamin A carotenoids are found primarily in fruits and vegetables. The most important of these is beta-carotene, though alpha-carotene and beta-cryptoxanthin also contribute.

Your body converts both carotenoids and preformed vitamin A into the active forms it needs, including retinal and retinoic acid.

Why do I need vitamin A during pregnancy?

Vitamin A is critical in many ways for your baby's development. Through its role in cell growth and division, it has a major influence on the development of your baby's cells, bones, skin, and eyes. Be sure to get the right amount of this super-nutrient, though: Too little has been linked to premature birth and slow growth, but too much through a supplement can be toxic.

How much vitamin A do I need during pregnancy?

Pregnant women 19 and older need 770 mcg RAE (retinol activity equivalents) of vitamin A per day. Pregnant women 18 and under need 750 mcg RAE.

In addition to helping you and baby convert carbs into energy, vitamin B1 also plays a critical role in baby's brain development.

Vitamin B2 (riboflavin)

Vitamin B2, or riboflavin, is a vitamin that's essential for growth and overall good health. It allows your body to use oxygen, and it's needed to break down carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. It's also used for the development and function of the skin, the lining of the digestive tract, blood cells, and other vital organs.

Why do I need vitamin B2 during pregnancy?

If you're in the second trimester or third trimester, your baby might already be a ball of energy, with sharp pokes and kicks causing you some sleepless nights. That's where riboflavin comes in handy, as it helps your body produce energy. Work a little more B2 into your diet for that much-needed boost. It also helps promote cell division and your baby's continued growth, as well as good vision and healthy skin for both of you.

Your body can also make niacin from the amino acid tryptophan, found in nuts, seeds, tofu, cheese, red meat, and chicken (among other foods).

Vitamin C

Also known as L-ascorbic acid, vitamin C is a water-soluble vitamin needed to make collagen, a vital protein that aids our blood vessels, cartilage, tendons, and bones.

Why do I need vitamin C during pregnancy?

Vitamin C is an essential nutrient you need every day, and pregnancy is no exception. Because your body can't produce vitamin C on its own, it's important to get enough through food and supplements.

This powerful nutrient supports both your health and your baby's development. Some studies have linked low vitamin C levels to an increased risk of preeclampsia, which may help explain why your vitamin C needs increase during pregnancy. Vitamin C also supports a healthy immune system and helps your body absorb iron, another key nutrient for expectant moms.

Vitamin D

Vitamin D is a fat-soluble vitamin that's produced in the skin when it's exposed to UV rays. Only found in a few foods, it helps the body absorb calcium and maintain normal mineralization of the bones. It's also important for many other functions, including cell growth, immune system health, and reduction of inflammation.

Why do I need vitamin D during pregnancy?

Vitamin D helps in the absorption of calcium, and, with so much baby bone-building going on during pregnancy, it's especially important. A deficiency of vitamin D during pregnancy can lead to rickets (softening of the bones), muscle disease, and other problems in newborns.

How much vitamin D do I need during pregnancy?

The suggested amount during pregnancy and breastfeeding is at least 600 IU a day. But it's not uncommon to need more, so check with your doctor about how much you should get.

Why do I need vitamin E during pregnancy?

Vitamin E helps your body form and use red blood cells and muscles, all crucial for your growing baby. While vitamin E deficiency isn't common in healthy adults, research has found that sufficient vitamin E during pregnancy can help reduce the chances of developing preeclampsia.

Vitamin K

Vitamin K is a fat-soluble vitamin that helps the body build protein for healthy bones and normal blood clotting. Vitamin K also works with vitamin D to ensure that calcium finds its way to the bones to help them develop properly.

Why do I need vitamin K during pregnancy?

Because vitamin K plays an important role in making your blood clot, it helps prevent blood loss after injury and, eventually, childbirth . (Since all babies are born with low levels of K, a single injection of vitamin K is given before leaving the hospital.)

How much vitamin K do I need during pregnancy?

There is no recommended dietary allowance for vitamin K, but the suggested adequate intake is 90 mcg for women. Discuss vitamin K supplements with your doctor before taking any, because they can cause adverse reactions when you're on certain medications.

Water

While water isn't a nutrient that provides calories, staying well hydrated throughout pregnancy is a must.

Why is water important during pregnancy?

Water helps keep the body hydrated and functioning at its peak. It also aids in delivering all the nutrients, vitamins, and minerals from the healthy food you eat and those prenatal vitamins you take to your growing baby. Water aids in everything from hormonal and temperature balance to digestion and absorption of nutrients. Sufficient fluids can help prevent UTIs, bladder infections, headaches , fatigue , constipation, and hemorrhoids .

How much water do I need during pregnancy?

It can vary widely depending on the person, so ask your practitioner what's right for you, but a general range is eight to 10 8-ounce glasses from all sources, including food.

Zinc

Zinc is considered an essential trace mineral because very small amounts of it are necessary for human health. It helps the immune system fight invading bacteria and viruses, and the body needs it for making proteins and DNA.

Why do I need zinc during pregnancy?

Getting enough zinc during pregnancy is important for cell growth and the creation of your little one's own genetic makeup. It also keeps you healthy by fighting illnesses if you get sick during pregnancy. In addition, inadequate zinc intake is linked to low-birthweight babies and other problems in labor and delivery.

How much zinc do I need during pregnancy?

During pregnancy you need 11 mg of zinc per day — and no more than 40 mg.

Meeting your pregnancy nutrition needs doesn't have to be complicated. Focusing on a variety of nutrient-dense foods can help ensure you're getting the vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats you and your growing baby need.

Since it can be difficult to get every essential nutrient from food alone, experts recommend taking a daily prenatal vitamin before conception and throughout pregnancy. Talk with your health care practitioner about which prenatal vitamin is right for you and whether you may need additional supplements based on your individual needs.

"My diet was so healthy before pregnancy and now I can barely eat," says Community member CorineeSJW. "So I make sure to take my prenatal every day."
