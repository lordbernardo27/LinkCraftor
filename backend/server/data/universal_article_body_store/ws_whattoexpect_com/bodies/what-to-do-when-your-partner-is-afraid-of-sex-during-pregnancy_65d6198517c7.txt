What to Do When Your Partner Is Afraid of Sex During Pregnancy

by Heidi Murkoff , author of What to Expect When You're Expecting.

Updates to copy, sources, and formatting.

Loving pregnant couple

"My husband used to want to have sex all the time, but now that I'm pregnant, he won't even touch me. I don't know what to do about it, but I wish he would take advantage of the fact that I'm feeling really turned on these days (oddly enough!). It seems like this would be a great time for both of us if he could just get past this."

Isn't it funny how the very same pregnancy hormones that make you bloated and queasy can also leave you hungry — for sex? (Though if you're a mom-to-be experiencing a decreased sex drive , that's perfectly normal too.)

But if you're always in the mood lately, there are good reasons for it. For one — your perpetually engorged pelvic region might contribute to an increased sex drive. For another, there's probably less stress between the sheets than there's been in a while — since you're not actively trying to prevent a baby or create one. (And, if you're a first-time mom, you're not tending to one yet either!)

All of which sounds like the perfect recipe for romance, except for one missing ingredient: your partner.

For some non-birthing partners, it's a matter of fear — it's possible your partner is just afraid of hurting you or hurting the baby. For others, it's the weirdness factor — getting close to you might mean getting too close for comfort to your baby during a decidedly adult activity.

Key Takeaways

Pregnancy can boost your libido thanks to hormonal changes and less stress about trying to conceive. But a high sex drive doesn't always mean your partner is on the same page.

Your partner's hesitation is usually about fear or discomfort, not a rejection of you. Open, honest conversations can help ease his worries and bring you closer.

There are plenty of ways to connect intimately beyond intercourse. Cuddling, massages, or exploring safe bedroom play can keep the romance alive during pregnancy.

"I went through this earlier in my pregnancy," recalls What to Expect Community mom Coffeeeahhh. "The fact that there is a baby in my tummy just freaked him out for a really long time when it came to sex, even after I explained that his swimmers weren't getting anywhere near the baby. We went for most of the first and second trimester without sex. He finally felt comfortable with it towards the middle/end of the second trimester."

Whatever the reason, remember not to take it personally — it's not a reflection on you, or a rejection of you. But don't resign yourself to a nine-month dry spell either. Instead, initiate some pillow talk. Tell your partner how you're feeling (all fired up and no place to go), and find out what's going on in his head.

Be understanding and patient if your partner has baggage he needs to unpack before you hit the sack again. Reassure him that pregnancy sex is perfectly safe (as long as your practitioner has given you the green light).

Hormones doing their thing? Download the What to Expect app for tips on managing all manner of pregnancy-related symptoms like mood swings, cravings, and everything in between.

Let your partner know (or let him read about it on the site or in What to Expect When You're Expecting ) that babies are completely oblivious to parental goings-on (and gettings-it-on), completely impervious to the proceedings, not within reach, and likely to be lulled to sleep by the gentle, rhythmic rocking of lovemaking and orgasm. If he's not biting, have your practitioner give him the "sex is safe (and good exercise)" talk.

If he's still not jumping on the jumping-you bandwagon, maybe the two of you could explore other sexual options that might inspire him — like visiting an adult store (online if you're shy) for some toys (the kind that won't be for the baby) to stimulate his interest. Or set the stage for romance — add some mood lighting and music, and offer up a massage (complete with scented oils).

Or if you're afraid that's too much pressure for your partner (and for you), just engage in some cozy cuddling on the sofa — it might just put him on the same passionate page as you, and make his hang-ups history.
