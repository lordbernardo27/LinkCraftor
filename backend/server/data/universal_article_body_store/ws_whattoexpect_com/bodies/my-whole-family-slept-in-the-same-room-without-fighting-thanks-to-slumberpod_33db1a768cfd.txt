My Whole Family Slept in the Same Room Without Fighting: This Viral "Blackout Pod" Saved Our Vacation

Our first attempt at room-sharing was a disaster until we tried this viral sleep tent.

Our first experience sharing a room as a family was a disaster. Olivia was four months old and technically a great sleeper, the kind of baby you have to wake to eat, so we assumed vacation sleep would be easy. It wasn’t. Every time we crept into the room, we woke her. A creaky floorboard or a flash of phone light was enough to set her off. And once she was back asleep, the problem flipped. Olivia is an “active sleeper,” so her midnight grunts and whimpers kept my husband up all night. By the second night, we were all exhausted and snapping at each other. I wasn’t looking forward to future trips, I was questioning them entirely.

Right on cue, my feed started serving me ads for the SlumberPod , a blackout “sleep tent” that mom influencers swear by. To me, it looked like a glorified tent, and I had questions. Would I hear her if she cried? Would it get too hot? Was this going to be a nightmare to set up?

Slumberpod set up in a guest room

After nearly four years of using it, I can confidently say: yes, no, and only the first time.

I brought it on a trip to Vermont, still skeptical. The first night, I tiptoed around like usual. The second night, we stopped trying. We put Olivia down, left the lights on, and watched a movie at a normal volume. She didn’t stir. She was in her own dark, quiet bubble while we were three feet away, finally relaxing. We used the built-in monitor pouch to keep an eye on her, but the pod blocked just enough light and sound that my husband actually slept through her usual nighttime noises.

The real test came the next morning. The room was flooded with early sunlight, the kind that usually guarantees a 5 a.m. wake-up. I crawled inside the pod and had my husband zip it up. Total darkness. Even with the open bottom, it blocked nearly all the light.

Leah Rocketto, the writer, inside the Slumberpod.

As for safety, it’s designed with airflow in mind. The fabric is breathable, and there’s a ventilation panel plus pockets for a small fan if you need it. I was worried about that going in, but it ended up being a non-issue.

The biggest drawback is setup. It’s not hard, but you do need space to bend the long poles into place. Do a test run at home and set it up as soon as you arrive; doing it while sleep-deprived is not the move. It’s also not cheap, usually around $180 to $200. But compared to booking a larger room or dealing with another trip like that first one, it paid for itself quickly.

Family vacations are rarely stress-free, but losing sleep doesn’t have to be part of it. The SlumberPod didn’t just help Olivia sleep, it gave us our evenings back.
