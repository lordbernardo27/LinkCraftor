Here's what you're paying for (it's not accuracy).

There's no need to spend more for a more expensive pregnancy test unless you're interested in certain extra features. All home pregnancy tests use the same method for detecting pregnancy, and when used correctly, they're 99% accurate. Paying more may get you a few added conveniences, such as a digital display or slightly shorter wait time for results.

Here's what to know about the differences between cheap and expensive pregnancy tests and how to decide what kind of pregnancy test is right for you.

Key Takeaways

All home pregnancy tests are equally accurate when used correctly. Whether a test costs $7 or $27, it can be up to 99% accurate when taken on or after the first day of your expected period.

More expensive tests offer convenience, not better accuracy. Higher-priced options may include features like digital displays, faster results, or more tests per package, but they don't provide more reliable results.

Timing matters more than price. Testing too early increases the chance of a false-negative result, even with the most sensitive tests. For the most reliable answer, wait until the day your period is due, regardless of which test you choose.

Is there a difference between expensive and cheap pregnancy tests?

There's no major difference between expensive and cheap pregnancy tests — especially when it comes to accuracy. All pregnancy tests work by measuring levels of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) , a pregnancy hormone produced by the placenta after a fertilized egg implants in the uterus.

HCG can be detected in both urine and blood, and levels rise rapidly during early pregnancy. When used correctly, most tests can detect the hormone in your urine on the first day of your expected period.

How much do cheap pregnancy tests cost vs. expensive ones?

Depending on the brand, sensitivity, and other features, you can expect to pay somewhere between $7 and $27 per package.

Are expensive tests more accurate than cheap ones?

No. All home pregnancy tests are up to 99% accurate if you follow the instructions. That’s just as reliable as the urine pregnancy tests used at doctors' offices.

The key is taking the test at the right time. You’ll get the best reading when you wait until the first day of your expected period.

Testing too early in your cycle, before enough hCG has built up in your urine, can lead to a false negative result. False positives can also occur, but they’re rare. Testing very early may also detect a chemical pregnancy , where a fertilized egg implants in the uterus but stops growing before it's big enough to see on an early ultrasound .

It may be possible that a more sensitive — and often more expensive — pregnancy test may be able to give you that all-important yes or no a little sooner. But waiting to use it until the expected day of your period will generally give you the most reliable result, regardless of how much the test cost.

In fact, even the fine print on tests that claim to give you results five to six days before your missed period say that they’re not 99% accurate until the day of your expected period. And because hCG levels keep increasing, every additional day you wait will make the test even more accurate.

What are the pros and cons of less expensive pregnancy tests?

The main benefit is — you guessed it — they’re cheaper! And they work just as well. The results of less expensive pregnancy tests will still be up to 99% accurate when you use them according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

As for the downsides? Cheaper options may have:

Fewer convenience features , such as plastic handles or a slightly shorter wait time.

Traditional, line-based displays instead of digital readouts. Some research suggests line-based tests may be harder for users to interpret correctly, especially when the line on the display is super faint .

Fewer tests per package . If you like the idea of being able to confirm your results by testing more than once, a larger package may be worth the extra cost.

"I want to keep peeing on the sticks to see the pink lines and be happy all over again until I get my first ultrasound," says What to Expect Community member agray10102020.

What are the pros and cons of pricier pregnancy tests?

The main drawback is the price.

The benefits may include:

Greater sensitivity to hCG levels , which means that you may be able to get an accurate result sooner. While some brands claim they can detect pregnancy as early as five to six days before your missed period, it’s not a guarantee. No matter how much you pay, testing before your expected period increases the chances of getting a false-negative result.

Faster results , so you'll know within a few seconds instead of a few minutes.

Extra bells and whistles , such as plastic handles and digital displays, which could make results easier to read and interpret.

More tests per package, which can be handy if you know you’ll want to double- (or triple-) check your results or have extra pregnancy tests on hand if you need to try again next month. Most packages include at least two tests, but some offer five or more.

What kind of pregnancy test is right for me?

Since all pregnancy tests work essentially the same way and are equally reliable when used correctly, the best choice comes down to your personal preferences.

If keeping costs low is your priority, one of the cheaper pregnancy tests might be a better choice.

"I've used all sorts of tests," says Community member missmamameow. "The dollar store ones are great."

If you're eager to test before the first day of your expected period, you may prefer a more sensitive test that can be used earlier — as long as you understand that the results may be less reliable than if you waited.

And if you like extra features that make taking and reading a pregnancy test easier, then it might be worth it to you to pay more for those.

Whatever pregnancy test you choose, take note of the expiration date on the box before buying it. Cheap pregnancy tests don’t expire faster than expensive ones, but any expired pregnancy test is more likely to give a false reading.

Rest assured that whether you spend $7 or $27 on a pregnancy test, your results will be equally accurate. And if you get a positive result, you'll need to follow up with your health care practitioner to confirm the pregnancy and begin prenatal care.
