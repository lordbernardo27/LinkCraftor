What is Septril Piercing?

A septril piercing is a hybrid between a septum and a rhino piercing. It goes through the bottom part of the septum and exits at the tip of the nose. Due to its position, the septum needs to be stretched so that the jewelry can be comfortably fitted.

This piercing is not for the faint of heart. It needs a specific technique, a trained professional, and a longer healing process. But for those willing to commit, the results can be stunning and truly one of a kind.

Let’s Talk About Cost

Source: Reddit

The cost of a septril piercing can vary significantly based on several factors, including location, the skill and experience of the piercer, and the type of jewelry you choose. On average, the price for the procedure is between $50 to $150. However, you should always consider some additional cost especially if your septum has not been previously stretched.

Stretching the septum is a process that takes time, and may require multiple visits to the piercer before the actual septril piercing can be performed. Moreover, the type of jewelry you choose can significantly affect the cost of the process. You can choose from titanium, surgical steel, gold, glass, or acrylic. Needless to say more precious the metal, the higher the cost.

Last but not least, aftercare can also add to the cost. While some studios include a basic saline solution in the piercing cost, others may charge extra for aftercare kits containing specialized antibacterial sprays, non-iodized sea salt, and healing balms.

Key Takeaways

If your septum hasn’t been stretched, multiple sessions may be required before getting a septril piercing, increasing both time and cost.

While basic jewelry may be included in the piercing fee, opting for premium materials like solid gold or custom designs can add to the overall expense.

Improper healing or complications may require additional consultations, potentially increasing the cost.

Investing in high-quality aftercare products supports proper healing but adds to the total cost of maintaining the piercing.

Is it Painful?

Source: Pinterest

Let’s be real, pain is subjective. Some people can tolerate piercings like champs, while others find them unbearable. That being said, the septril piercing is considered one of the more painful nose piercings because of a few reasons, such as:

Pre-stretched septum : If your septum has already been stretched, it’s likely more sensitive which can make the piercing process more painful.

Thicker cartilage : Unlike a simple nostril or septum piercing, the septril involves puncturing dense cartilage , which naturally leads to more pressure and pain.

Multi-step process : A septril isn’t a one-and-done deal; it’s typically performed over several stretching sessions , increasing discomfort over time.

On a scale of 1 to 10, most people rate the pain between 6 to 8 . However, the pain is usually short-lived and usually lasts only during the procedure itself. However, there is not one answer that fits all. In other cases, pain can extend for a few days whereas in case of infection, the pain can last for weeks.

Nonetheless, under normal circumstances, the pain and discomfort after the procedure are manageable with proper aftercare.

Are There Any Risks to Septril Piercing?

Like any piercing, a septril piercing comes with its own set of risks. You need to learn about these risks so that you can make an informed decision. Here’s what you need to know:

Infection : If not cleaned properly, bacteria can enter the piercing site which can lead to to redness, swelling, pain, and discharge . Furthermore, severe infections may even cause abscess formation or require medical treatment.

Scarring : Due to its placement, improper healing or removal of the jewelry too soon can leave noticeable scars on the nose. Keloids are thick, raised scars that may develop in some individuals.

Blowouts : When stretching the septum too quickly, tissue can tear or thicken , forming a bulging ring of scar tissue around the hole.

To minimize these risks , you should always choose an experienced piercer , follow proper aftercare guidelines , and most importantly, try avoiding the piercing until it’s healed.

How Long Does it Take to Heal?

Healing time for a septril piercing varies from person to person, but it generally takes around 6 months to a year. However, several factors can affect healing time, including:

Your Body’s Natural Healing Speed : Healing varies from person to person. Some people heal faster than others due to genetics, overall health, and immune system function . So, if your body is taking longer to heal, don’t worry about it.

Proper Aftercare : Properly cleaning the piercing regularly can significantly reduce the healing time of your piercing. Use saline solution and avoid any harsh chemicals like alcohol or hydrogen peroxide.

The Size of the Stretched Septum Before Piercing : If your septum wasn’t stretched properly beforehand , healing may take longer because your body has to adjust to both the stretched tissue and the new piercing.

Key Takeaways:

Healing time for a septril piercing ranges from 6 months to a year.

Your body’s natural healing speed plays a role, some people heal faster due to genetics and immune health.

Proper aftercare with saline solution and avoiding harsh chemicals can help speed up healing and prevent infections.

The size of the stretched septum before piercing impacts healing; if stretching isn’t done properly, the process may take longer.

Patience is key. Rushing the healing process can lead to complications like irritation or scarring.

How to Care for It After the Piercing?

As we discussed above, proper aftercare is crucial to avoid infections and speed up healing. Here are some tips you can use to ensure your piercing heals properly:

Do:

Clean the piercing with a saline solution twice a day (a simple mix of non-iodized sea salt and warm water works wonders).

Wash your hands before touching your piercing. Hands are full of germs and can cause severe infections.

Use a mild soap when washing your face to avoid irritation.

Be patient and allow your body to heal naturally.

Keep your piercing dry.

Don't:

Avoid alcohol-based solutions or hydrogen peroxide. Such harsh solutions can dry out the piercing.

Don’t touch or twist the jewelry unnecessarily.

Avoid swimming in public pools, lakes, or oceans until fully healed.

Don’t rush the stretching process, it’s a long game and patience goes a long way.

What About Jewelry?

Choosing the right jewelry for a septril piercing is important for both comfort and aesthetics. Here are some popular options:

Materials to Consider:

Titanium : It is hypoallergenic and ideal for sensitive skin. It is lightweight, and resistant to corrosion which makes it perfect for long-term wear.

Surgical Steel : It is durable and affordable but may cause irritation in individuals with nickel allergies. It's a good choice for initial piercing however, you should consider changing it once your piercing has matured.

Gold : You must at least use 14K gold to avoid infections, as lower karat gold contains alloys that may cause irritation.

Glass or Acrylic : It is a good option for temporary wear but not recommended for fresh piercings due to their porous nature. It can harbor bacteria which can ultimately cause infections.

Jewelry Styles:

Flat Disks – Small and discreet, ideal for an understated look.

Tiny Studs – Add a bit of sparkle without overwhelming the face.

Tunnels or Plugs – If you’ve stretched your septum significantly, a tunnel can create a cool aesthetic.

Bent Barbells – Commonly used for a fresh septril piercing.

Make sure your jewelry is high-quality and properly fitted to avoid irritation and rejection.
