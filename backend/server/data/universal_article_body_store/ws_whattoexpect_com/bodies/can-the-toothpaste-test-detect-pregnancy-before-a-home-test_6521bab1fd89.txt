Google "home pregnancy test" and you'll find lots of DIY hacks using ingredients like salt, sugar, soap, bleach, or baking soda. There's one more substance that can supposedly tell you whether you're pregnant: toothpaste.

You can try the toothpaste pregnancy test for fun, but don't count on the results! Mixing a little urine with toothpaste is said to reveal whether you're pregnant based on color changes or foaming. There's no scientific basis for accuracy, but there's no harm in the experiment, either.

Here's what to know about the toothpaste pregnancy test, including how it supposedly works and whether it's accurate.

Key Takeaways

The toothpaste pregnancy test is not scientifically reliable. Although some people claim that mixing urine with toothpaste can indicate pregnancy through foaming or color changes, there is no evidence that this method accurately detects pregnancy.

Any reaction is likely caused by urine acidity, not pregnancy hormones. The test is based on the theory that the pregnancy hormone hCG reacts with toothpaste ingredients, but normal urine is already acidic and can produce the same foaming effect, whether a person is pregnant or not.

A store-bought pregnancy test is the best way to get early, accurate results. Home pregnancy tests are designed to detect hCG and can be up to 99% accurate when used correctly.

What is the toothpaste pregnancy test?

The toothpaste pregnancy test is a DIY method that involves adding a few drops of urine to toothpaste. According to the theory, the resulting chemical reaction can indicate whether you're expecting. However, the test isn't scientifically sound.

How do you take the toothpaste pregnancy test?

Here's how to take the DIY toothpaste pregnancy test, according to various YouTube tutorials:

Squeeze a layer of white toothpaste to fill the bottom of a small cup.

Add a few drops of urine (how many drops seems to be up for debate).

Stir the mixture.

Watch to see if the toothpaste foams or changes color.

How does the toothpaste pregnancy test work?

In theory, the acids in a pregnant woman's urine cause toothpaste to foam. The acidity of a non-pregnant person's urine, however, could have the same effect — making this little experiment interesting but not dependable.

When you're pregnant, your body produces human chorionic gonadotropin, aka hCG . This hormone, which is made up of amino acids, is what regular home pregnancy tests and your doctor are looking for in your urine and blood.

Toothpaste contains a chemical called calcium carbonate, an abrasive commonly used to scrub the surface of the teeth. Some claim that when the calcium carbonate in the toothpaste combines with the amino acids in hCG, it produces carbon dioxide gas. In other words, it foams.

There's a catch: Normal urine also contains uric acid and tends to be slightly acidic, with a normal pH range of 4.6 to 8.0 (a pH less than 7.0 is acidic). That means the uric acid in your pee could cause the same reaction whether or not you're pregnant.

As for the claim that hCG turns toothpaste blue, there doesn't really seem to be evidence that always happens — or a scientific explanation as to why it would.

How accurate is the toothpaste pregnancy test?

While it might be a fun chemistry experiment (if you're into that kind of thing), the toothpaste pregnancy test is not an accurate or reliable way to tell whether or not you're pregnant.

When should you take the toothpaste pregnancy test?

If you're curious, it's fine to take a toothpaste pregnancy test anytime. Of course, if you want a meaningful result, use a home pregnancy test instead.

For the most accurate results, wait at least until the day that your period is supposed to show up. At that point, there should be enough hCG in your urine to be detectable. Also, try to take the test in the morning, when the hCG in your urine is most concentrated.

How do I read the toothpaste pregnancy test results?

After stirring the toothpaste and the urine together, watch to see if the solution foams or changes color. If it does, you're pregnant — according to the rumors, anyway. But remember, the results are only for fun. A toothpaste test can't actually confirm that you're pregnant.

What does a positive toothpaste pregnancy test look like?

If you're expecting, the toothpaste will supposedly begin to foam or turn blue (or both). But the mixture in many online video tutorials doesn't look particularly blue — and remember that there's no scientific reason it would change colors.

Keep in mind that the uric acid in your pee could cause the toothpaste to foam even if you're not pregnant.

What does a negative toothpaste pregnancy test look like?

If you're not expecting, the toothpaste supposedly won't foam or turn blue. But since urine is typically acidic, there's a good chance the mixture will foam either way.

Bottom line: If you think you might be expecting, your best bet is still to use an approved, store-bought at-home pregnancy test rather than any of the DIY options out there.

These tests are specifically designed to detect hCG and can identify pregnancy as early as six to 12 days after fertilization, though results are most accurate after a missed period. Many home pregnancy tests claim to be up to 99% accurate.

Once you've taken a legitimate home pregnancy test and gotten a positive result, you can confirm the pregnancy with a blood test at your OB/GYN's office, which definitively tells you whether or not your body is producing hCG — and a baby.
