Pink Noise vs. White Noise for Baby Sleep? I Put Hatch’s New Sound Machine to the Test

The Hatch Baby, the newest white noise machine and night light from Hatch.

After using the original Hatch Rest with my first baby, I tested the new Hatch Baby and its pink noise feature in her room to see how it stacks up.

Tim and I have been Hatch devotees for years. Long before we had kids, we relied on the brand’s Restore in our own bedroom for its wind-down routines, sunrise alarm, and night-long white noise. So when Lou was born, adding the original Hatch Rest sound machine to her nursery felt like an obvious move. We plugged it in on day one, and it’s been a core part of her bedtime routine, helping her sleep consistently through the night.

Now that baby #2 is arriving in June, I’m getting our nursery gear ready again. Hatch recently launched an updated version of its popular sound machine, now called Hatch Baby . One of the biggest updates is that it now includes pink noise, a softer sound frequency that many parents say helps babies sleep more deeply. I swapped Lou’s original Hatch Rest for the new model to see how it compares.

After testing it for a few nights, I already have some thoughts. First, here’s the new model we’ve been using in Lou’s room.

Hatch Baby

Nursery Upgrade

Hatch Baby Looks Like a Tiny Chic Lamp

The first thing I noticed about the new Hatch Baby is the design. The original Hatch Rest is perfectly functional, but it definitely looks like baby gear. The new version, especially in the Putty color I chose, looks more like a tiny sculptural lamp.

It’s rounder, softer, and genuinely cuter than its more gadget-like predecessor, with the charming ability to blend in as nursery decor. Hatch also offers the device in Mint, Peach, and Greige, which makes it easier to match different nursery styles. If you, like me, care about how baby gear looks in your home, this upgrade alone is noticeable.

Hatch Baby vs. Hatch Rest: What’s Different

We’ve used the original Hatch Rest in Lou’s room since day one, so I noticed a few differences immediately when I swapped in the new model.

The design is a major upgrade: The new version looks more like a modern nursery lamp than a sound machine, with softer curves and a more elevated finish.

The sound quality feels fuller: White noise is the feature we use most, and the updated speaker sounds noticeably richer. The original worked well, but the new version makes the white noise feel deeper and less tinny.

The top button is easier to use: A large “Big Button” on top lets you start baby’s routine or adjust volume with a quick tap. The original model used a flat ring that could be surprisingly finicky, so this update is much easier to use during middle-of-the-night wakeups.

6 months of Hatch+ sleep support is included: The new Hatch Baby comes with six months of Hatch+, which unlocks predictive sleep schedules, milestone-based sleep guides, a library of premium stories and songs, and even a 1:1 chat with certified sleep consultants. The original Hatch Rest includes basic sounds and lights, but accessing the full content library requires a paid Hatch+ subscription.

A 30-night nursery trial: Hatch is also offering a 30-night nursery trial, so you can test the sound machine in your baby’s room and return it within 30 days if it’s not the right fit.

The Hatch Baby Features We Already Loved

Importantly, the new model keeps the things that made the original Hatch Rest so useful in the first place.

You still get:

Continuous white noise that runs all night (we’ve used this since Lou was a newborn)

A customizable nightlight that’s soft enough for middle-of-the-night feedings and diaper changes

App-controlled routines and schedules so you can adjust sound and light without walking into the room

Those core features are exactly why the Hatch Rest has stayed part of Lou’s bedtime routine for nearly two years.

Why Pink Noise and Other Sound Options Matter

White noise has long been a nursery staple, though lately it’s become a trending debate among parents and sleep experts about volume levels and long-term reliance. One thing I’ve always appreciated about Hatch is that it offers more than just traditional white noise. The original Hatch Rest includes brown noise and gentle nature sounds like ocean waves, while the new Hatch Baby adds pink noise, a softer, more balanced frequency often compared to steady rainfall. We’ve always kept the ocean setting running through the night, and it now signals bedtime almost instantly.

How the Price Compares

The new Hatch Baby is priced at $99.99 , while the original Hatch Rest is still available through some retailers, like Amazon, for around $69.99 . The new version also includes six months of Hatch+ sleep support, which adds guided sleep tools and extra content compared with the original device.

Hatch has stopped selling the legacy Rest on its own website, replacing it with the new Hatch Baby model, though the original device can still be found through third-party retailers.

The Verdict

Bottom line: Hatch Baby is a worthy upgrade to the Hatch Rest, with a softer sound profile, pink noise, and a design that looks much nicer in a nursery.

After a few nights of testing the new Hatch Baby in Lou’s room, I’m convinced it’s a worthy successor to the original Hatch Rest we’ve relied on since she was a newborn. It keeps everything we loved about the original, but looks nicer on the dresser and adds a few thoughtful upgrades.

Lou seems perfectly happy with the switch, which is about the best product review you can get from a toddler. And when baby #2 arrives in June, the new Hatch Baby will already be warmed up and ready to take over nursery duty from day one.
