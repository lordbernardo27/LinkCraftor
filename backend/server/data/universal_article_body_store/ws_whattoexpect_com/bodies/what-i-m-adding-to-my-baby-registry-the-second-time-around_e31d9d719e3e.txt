What I’m Adding to My Baby Registry the Second Time Around

Second Baby Registry Tips: A pregnant person opening a gift at a baby shower

Here’s what I regret skipping the first time and what I won’t bother with for Baby #2.

Just so you know, What to Expect may earn commissions from the shopping links included on this page. All sales and prices are accurate at the time of publishing.

Hi all! I’m Leah, a What to Expect editor, casual-turned-professional deal hunter and mom to a 3-year-old named Olivia. I also have another baby girl on the way. Now that I’m well into my second trimester, my second baby registry is officially underway.

This time, I’m bringing both my editorial experience (over a decade researching and writing about baby gear) and the real-life lessons that come from surviving the newborn phase.

I’ve been thinking about the sanity-saving items I bought in a panic (portable night lights were a surprise win), the so-called must-haves that collected dust (I was overly ambitious about mastering a wrap carrier) and the tried-and-true gear I used nonstop and plan to use again.

Here’s what I’ll be registering for before Baby #2 arrives — and what I’ve learned to skip.

Baby Gear Worth the Investment

Mockingbird Single-to-Double Stroller : Sleek, easy to maneuver and just as versatile as higher-end strollers (but at a fraction of the price).

Chicco Fit360 Rotating Car Seat : This car seat works from day one through the booster stage. The rotating base was especially helpful while recovering from delivery.

mamaRoo Multi-Motion Baby Swing : This swing kept Olivia calm and soothed. It even earned the nickname “mamaPoo” in our house thanks to its magical effect on baby digestion.

Fisher-Price 3-in-1 Sensory Gym : A true tummy-time MVP. The six repositionable toys kept Olivia engaged through a tough milestone.

Ergobaby Mesh Baby Carrier : Comfortable, lightweight and easy to use even in those sleep-deprived early days.

Nursery Essentials

The Ollie Swaddle : The only swaddle I could manage while half-asleep under dim nightlight. Simple and secure.

Nanit Pro Baby Monitor + Floor Stand : Clear video and helpful sleep insights gave us peace of mind and helped us build a loose nap schedule.

Hatch Rest Sound Machine : Customizable sound and light settings that even my mom could figure out.

Feeding Must-Haves

Nuk Simply Natural Baby Bottles : Designed to support combo feeding, with multiple holes for a natural flow and a wide neck for easier latch.

The First Years Modular Steam Sterilizer : After many late nights boiling bottles on the stove, my mom gifted me this compact, time-saving model.

Konny Baby Bibs : Cute enough to be worn as an outfit accessory, but super absorbent too.

Comfy Cubs Muslin Burp Cloths : Soft, durable and affordable. I kept a stash in every room.

Boppy Nursing Pillow : This helped with positioning during breastfeeding and doubled as a tummy time support pillow.

ChookaChoo Teething Mittens : Most teethers don’t stay in your baby’s hands, but these actually do.

Changing Table MVPs

WaterWipes : A clean formula that’s gentle on sensitive skin and easy to toss in the diaper bag.

Tubby Todd All-Over Ointment : Great for diaper rash, eczema and dry skin. A little goes a long way.

Bumco Baby Bum Brush : This silicone spatula kept my hands mess-free, especially when we were on the go.

Oogiebear Nose and Ear Cleaner : The soft silicone loop made it easy to remove dried boogies and ear wax without a fight.

haakaa Baby Nail File : Less nerve-wracking than clippers. The built-in light made trimming feel more precise.

Bath Time Basics

Aveeno Baby Body Wash and Lotion : Gentle and effective, especially for Olivia’s sensitive skin. I’ve been an Aveeno fan for years.

KeaBabies Hooded Towels : Some of the thickest and softest towels I’ve found. They made post-bath drying quick and cozy.

Frida Baby Grow-With-Me Tub : Smart design that grows with your baby and doesn’t take over the bathroom.

Small (but Mighty) Sanity Savers

Miss Mouth’s Messy Eater Stain Treater : This miracle spray tackled every stain, from blowouts to spaghetti nights. I use it on my clothes too.

Skip Hop Elbow Saver and Kneeler : Made long baths on cold tile much more bearable.

Anico EASZZZ Baby Night Light : Compact and portable with a soft glow that’s ideal for late-night feeds and changes.

Yogasleep Hushh Portable Sound Machine : Clips onto the stroller or crib and blocks out noise anywhere we go.

What I’m Skipping This Time

Button-Up Pajamas : They’re cute, but impractical. Zippers and magnets are much easier in the middle of the night.

Wipe Warmers : Not worth the space, and your baby won’t mind room-temperature wipes for long.

Bottle Warmers : We used a mug of warm water instead. Worked just fine.

Wrap Carriers : Tried one. Got tangled. Gave up.

Too Many Toys : Babies mostly sleep, eat, and poop. A few toys for tummy time and car rides are plenty.

Final Thoughts

Even with years of product research under my belt, I still got a few things wrong the first time. But those lessons made curating this second registry easier, smarter, and more realistic.

I hope my experience helps make your registry process a little smoother too. In the end, trust your instincts — you're a mom, after all. You know what your growing family needs most.
