Long before she's able to field a pass or throw a perfect spiral, your toddler makes a rewarding playmate: Her delighted grin at her first catch-and-hug will more than make up for all the time spent throwing the ball to her again and again.

When will my baby or toddler learn to roll a ball?

Around 12 to 15 months is when most babies start to enjoy rolling a ball on the floor (though some may start to experiment with this skill even earlier). This fun activity helps build finger strength, encourages visual tracking , and supports hand-eye coordination, all while introducing the key concept of "back and forth" (as opposed to "this is mine now!").

But even though your tot probably won't be rolling a ball on her own until sometime around age 1 , you can still incorporate balls into her playtime earlier.

Around 4 to 6 months, babies are working on reaching for and grabbing toys they’re interested in, and a colorful ball is an excellent object to place in her view. At this age, you can also help promote visual tracking by rolling a ball across the floor — she’ll likely start to turn her head to follow its path. When will my baby or toddler be able to throw a ball?

Many toddlers start throwing sometime around 15 to 18 months . A true overhand throw typically happens later, sometime around age 2 to 3.

While your little one may begin exercising this new skill by simply hurling toys across the room, a little coaching can go a long way. And it’s more than just a fun game — throwing helps your little one practice her fine motor and visual motor skills and further develop her hand-eye coordination. When will my baby or toddler learn to catch a ball?

Catching a ball comes a little later — around age 3 or 4. Most toddlers will make their first catch hugging the ball to the chest (a larger ball is a good one to start with); it typically takes lots and lots of practice to master the hand-eye coordination, reaction speed, and gross motor skills required to grab a flying ball first, then hug it to the body.

Tips for helping my child learn to play ball

Want to help your child perfect her throw-and-catch technique? Try these tips to help your little one learn to play ball:

Practice makes perfect. Give your little one ample opportunity to practice playing ball. Repetition builds familiarity, so keep on bouncing, tossing and chasing after runaway balls together.

Provide plenty of positive reinforcement. Don’t forget to cheer her on by clapping and showing lots of excitement for this new game you can play together.

Know when it's quitting time. Once your tot loses interest in playing ball, don't push her. Give it a break until next time.

Be patient and persistent. Remember, progress is never linear — your 3- or 4-year-old may excitedly catch and throw a ball one day, and be less interested in playing with it the next. Just keep at it!

Ball games to play with your baby or toddler

There are endless options when it comes to playing ball with your little one, but these simple games can help you get started.

I roll, you roll. Sit on the floor across from your child. Roll the ball toward her slowly, narrating what you’re doing (I’m rolling the ball to you!). Then encourage her to roll it back while you continue to narrate (Now you roll the ball back to me!). Half of the fun will be letting her chase after the ball when it goes off-course! Target practice. When it's time to graduate from rolling to throwing, let your little one practice with different kinds of tossables, from bean bags and squishy foam balls to beach balls and even a ring toss. Set up a line of buckets, containers, or bins for target practice. Move some closer and some farther away, and let your little one try sinking a few.

Catch it if you can! To practice catching, start out at your toddler's level, either sitting or on your knees, and toss the ball gently to her from a short distance away. If your ball is one that bounces, try bouncing the ball to her, which gives her a bit more time to react. Mix up your practice by throwing different sized balls — some smaller, some larger — from a variety of distances.

Ball safety tips for your baby or toddler

As with any type of play, safety comes first. Follow these tips to ensure your game of catch is age-appropriate for your tot:

Choose the right type of ball. If your little one is under age 4, make sure the ball she’s playing with is at least 1.75 inches in diameter — anything smaller could be a potential choking hazard , as could anything spongey she could take a bite out of.

Keep small parts out of reach. If your baby or toddler has older siblings who play with toys that contain marbles or spongey items, be sure to store them out of your younger child’s reach.

Set some ground rules. It helps if you demonstrate how to roll, throw and catch a ball safely. Set rules about safe play so no one gets hurt (and nothing gets broken!).

Should I be concerned if my child isn’t playing ball by a certain age?

Every child develops at her own pace. Even if your toddler isn't throwing a ball by 18 months, it's not a cause for concern as long as you're giving her plenty of opportunities for gross motor play. It may take lots of practice before your little one gets the hang of throwing and eventually catching a ball.

But feel free to take off your coaching hat whenever your little one is ready to call it quits for the day. Some children are naturally interested in athletics and will happily play toss-and-catch until your arms are sore, whereas others would rather go a couple of rounds and then play with something else.

During the toddler years, it makes more sense to let your little one call the game than to force her to practice. Because your tot’s mind and muscles are still developing — very much on her own unique timeline — it won't necessarily help her learn to catch or throw any faster if she's forced to play ball for longer than she wants to.

And remember: You can always check in with your pediatrician if you’re at all concerned about the timing and nature of any developmental milestones.

Most importantly, playing ball is a fun way to bond with your baby or toddler while helping to develop her gross motor skills and hand-eye coordination. Change things up to keep it engaging, and learn to read your little one’s cues so you know when it’s time for that seventh inning stretch!
