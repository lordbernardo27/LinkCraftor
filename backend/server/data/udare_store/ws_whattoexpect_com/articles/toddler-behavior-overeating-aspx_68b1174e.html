Your toddler’s appetite can change as he grows. Here’s what to do if you have questions and how to ensure he’s getting the nutrition he needs.

When the toddler years hit, some parents notice their tots’ appetites easing off, with some turning into ratherpicky eatersat mealtime. Other preschoolers have no problem cleaning their plates, leaving you to wonder what to make of your toddler’s appetite.

"Do I need to worry about overfeeding my 13-month-old?" asks What to Expect Community user rtm1014. "She has an amazing appetite and eats a ton. She often seems to eat more than my almost 4-year-old daughter. Do toddlers this age know when to stop eating or should I cut her off when it seems like it’s getting to be a lot? I always thought the former, but I am starting to worry a bit. I don’t know if I’m overthinking it."

Read on to learn how kids’ eating habits change as they grow and develop, as well as about toddlers who are always hungry and what to do if your child seems to want to eat all the time.

Key Takeaways

  • Toddlers’ appetites can vary. Growth spurts, high activity levels, and individual metabolism can cause some toddlers to eat more than others or show fluctuating hunger from day to day.
  • Overeating isn’t usually a concern if your child is healthy. Your pediatrician can help determine if your toddler’s intake is appropriate based on age, weight, growth, and activity.
  • Encourage healthy eating habits, such as by offering balanced meals and snacks, letting toddlers self-regulate portions, avoiding pressure to clean plates, introducing varied foods, and promoting physical activity.

Is my child overeating?

Your best resource for determining if your toddler is eating too much is your pediatrician, because they will consider different factors like your child’s age, height, weight, activity levels, and medical conditions, and make recommendations specific to him. Why is my child always hungry?

A busy toddler uses lots of energy to run, climb, and explore, and he needs nutritious food to fuel all the fun he’s having. Shoot forthree regular meals and two snackseach day to satisfy his appetite.

But keep in mind that kids’ development doesn’t progress at a smooth steady pace in childhood. In fact, mini growth spurts are to be expected in the toddler years, which in turn means you might notice a greater appetite in your child from time to time. What to do if your child wants to eat all the time

If you’re concerned about your toddler’s eating habits, check in with a pediatrician before making any changes to his diet. Your doctor can help answer any questions you have and offer advice on how to establish healthy habits for your family. In general, though, try these tips for snacktime, mealtime, and in between: Trusted SourceKidsHealth From NemoursNutrition Guide for ToddlersSee All Sources [4]

  • Set an example. Choosehealthy snacksand meals yourself and keep fruits, veggies, and other good-for-you choices on hand for everyone in the family to enjoy.
  • Skip the pressure. Fighting over each bite or fixating on the amount he eats is counterproductive. And avoid subscribing to the "clean plate" club since this doesn’t teach your child to stop eating when he feels full.
  • Train those tiny taste buds. Introduce your tot to a wide variety of healthy foods by filling the daily menu with whole grains, lean protein, fruits, veggies, and low-fat dairy products (after age 2).
  • Start with a healthy breakfast. Kids who regularly eat breakfast are more likely to get their daily nutrients, be at a healthy body weight, have better concentration, and miss fewer school days. Good toddler breakfast ideas include fruit, toast with peanut butter, eggs, and oatmeal. Trusted SourceKidsHealth From NemoursBreakfast BasicsSee All Sources [5]
  • Schedule snacks. Nutritious snacks help toddlers refuel between meals. Offer a snack between breakfast and lunch and another between lunch and dinner. More than that can curb his appetite at mealtime.
  • Help him heed hunger signals. Toddlers who are spoon-fed often end up eating more than they want or need, soteach your child to feed himself, and you'll be encouraging him to take control over how much he wants to eat. When he seems satisfied, let him leave the table.
  • Encourage exercise. Provide your child with plenty of opportunities for physical activity. And lead the way — when you get the whole family involved (walking, swimming, playing tag, bicycling), everyone reaps the benefits.

You know your child better than anyone, so take a good look at what he eats during the day and then discuss it with your pediatrician. Together, you can come up with some smart strategies for healthy eating for your toddler at both meals and snack time.
