What It’s Like Using the Skylight Calendar

Part of Skylight’s appeal is its easy-to-use design. A large display screen and clearly labeled tabs make toggling between tasks simple — so much so, even younger family members can get in on the action. I, however, prefer to use the accompanying app so I can add and edit things on the go.

Pro tip: Put your Skylight in a highly trafficked area so no one misses out — ours lives on the kitchen wall, where the family frequently hangs.

On top of the intuitive display, Skylight has several features that add to its appeal.

Calendar and Scheduling

Skylight Calendar_Calendar Mode

Rather than being another calendar to maintain, Skylight syncs with existing Google, Apple, and other major calendar platforms. This means everyone’s individual calendars come together in one place for easier scheduling. Plus, each family member has a color-coded profile, so it's clear who is busy and when.

Tasks and Routines

The Tasks feature was the real game-changer for our family.

Using the same color-coded system, you can designate one-time or recurring “chores” to a person. (You can even assign them a time of day to help develop routines.) Sounds simple, but some elements make it more appealing than a written checklist, especially for kids.

Skylight Calendar_Chores Mode

To start, parents can award a star value to tasks and corresponding rewards. Skylight tracks the number of stars so kids know when they can cash them in. Additionally, tasks can be labeled with large emojis, so children who aren’t reading yet still have some autonomy over their to-do list. This has also taken a lot of pressure off me to manage everything verbally.

My 3-year-old is noticeably more motivated to complete her routine now that she can check them off herself and watch the celebration animation at the end when everything is complete.

Meal Planning

Meals appear directly on the calendar with their own label and color. I no longer have to remember what we planned or open another app to check.

Recipes can be attached to meals, and ingredients can be added to a grocery list if you want everything in one place.

Worth Noting

One downside of Skylight is having to toggle between the calendar and tasks tab. Since only one can be displayed, we sometimes miss tasks if the calendar is front and center.

Another minor annoyance: Tasks with due dates only appear on the day they are due, which can be limiting for things that require advance prep. Skylight releases frequent updates and is responsive to user feedback, so these feel like solvable issues rather than dealbreakers.

Is the Premium Subscription Worth It?

The Skylight works great without a subscription, providing you with calendar functionality and a streamlined version of the task feature. But the paid Calendar Plus plan unlocks its best features, like reward charts, meal planning, and photo albums.

The $79/year subscription also gives you access to Sidekick, which uses AI to turn emails, PDFs, photos, and voice notes into calendar events or task lists. I forward daycare emails, upload school supply lists, and dictate packing lists, and everything appears automatically on the screen.

For me, the subscription is worth it for Sidekick alone.

Is the Skylight Calendar Worth It?

The Skylight Calendar is an investment, but it’s one I would make again. It’s taken a significant amount of mental load off my plate, making household responsibilities visible to everyone. Our home feels calmer, and I no longer feel solely responsible for remembering everything.

If shared calendars and basic to-do lists work for your family, this may be more than you need.
