Can you get pregnant from precum?

Is it possible to get pregnant from precum? Learn what pre-ejaculate is and what your chances are of getting pregnant from it.

If you’re trying to get pregnant — or avoid pregnancy — "pre-ejaculate" is an important word to know. Here's what you need to understand about its role in conception.

Key Takeaways

  • Pre-cum may have sperm. This substance isn’t semen, but it can still contain those little swimmers from the urethra, which means you could potentially get pregnant from it.
  • The pull-out method has to be perfectly timed to work. Truth: Men can’t feel when pre-ejaculate releases, which is why withdrawing to avoid pregnancy fails 20% of the time.
  • Not ready for a baby? Pick more effective birth control. Condoms, the pill, or a long-lasting IUD are all much more protective than relying on the withdrawal method alone.

What is pre-ejaculate (precum)?

Pre-ejaculate, also called precum, is a clear, thick fluid that comes out of a man's penis when he's aroused. This usually happens before he ejaculates.

Unlike actual semen, pre-ejaculate doesn't come from the testes but instead from the Cowper's gland — two pea-sized glands located right beneath the prostate gland. Pre-ejaculate may act like a lubricant during sex, and it also makes semen less watery, creating a conducive living environment for sperm.

Can you get pregnant from pre-ejaculate?

In a word, yes. Pre-ejaculate may contain sperm, which means you could get pregnant even if your partner doesn't fully ejaculate into your vagina.

Can you get pregnant if the man pulls out before ejaculating?

Again, yes. Most men can't tell when they've pre-ejaculated, and withdrawal itself takes a lot of self-control, which can be quite difficult when you're in the heat of the moment. And if even a few drops of semen land on the outside of your vagina, sperm may still swim in, which can lead to pregnancy.

What to Expect Communitymember ftm2722 knows full well that pre-ejactulate can be a roll of the dice. “It works until it doesn’t. My ex and I used pull-out successfully for 3 years — then I accidentally got pregnant twice in 6 months,” she shares.

It's also important to remember that "pulling out" doesn't protect you against sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Some STIs, like genital warts and herpes, are spread through skin-to-skin contact, while others, such as chlamydia, syphilis, or gonorrhea, can be carried in precum. So if you're worried about any infection, make sure that you use a condom.

If you're actively trying to avoid pregnancy, the main argument for withdrawal is that it keeps some semen out of the vagina. Other forms of birth control, like condoms, are more effective (but not foolproof). You can combine the two for a more potent pregnancy protection, or consider a more reliable method, such as thebirth control pillor theIUD.

What are your odds of getting pregnant from pre-ejaculate?

It's impossible to know for sure, as it can vary from person to person and with each sexual encounter. The best data available are studies that look at the withdrawal method. If your partner "perfects" the pull-out method, then your chances are relatively low: only about 4%.

But the reality is that doesn't always happen, which is why the "real life" effectiveness is only about 22%. That means about 22 out of 100 people who use the withdrawal method get pregnant every year, or roughly one in five.

By comparison, the IUD has a failure rate of less than 1%, while methods like the pill or patch are under 10%, and condoms are under 20%.

Bottom line? Pre-ejaculate is still ejaculate, which means it most likely contains sperm. If you're currently trying to avoid pregnancy, your best bet is to use a more reliable method of birth control.
