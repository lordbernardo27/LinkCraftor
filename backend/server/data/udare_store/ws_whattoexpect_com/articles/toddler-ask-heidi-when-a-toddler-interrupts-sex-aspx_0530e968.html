When a Toddler Interrupts Sex

by Heidi Murkoff , author of What to Expect When You're Expecting.| January 15, 2019

Ask Heidi Murkoff

"My toddler walked in on my husband and me having sex! What do I do? What do I say to her?"

First of all, relax (even though your intimate moment was anything but relaxing). Toddlers are fairly oblivious, and they’re especially unaware when they’re sleepy (which is good news if your little one burst in during an evening romp). Even though you’re well aware of what was going on, she hasn’t a clue that she walked in on you having sex. How could she? She doesn’t even know what sex is!

But she may know that she saw something after she interrupted sex. So, once your clothes are back on, try to uncover her feelings. Was she scared that you and your hubby were hurting each other? Stumped as to why you two were wrestling when she’s not allowed to roughhouse in the house? If so, address her fear and confusion head-on. Explain that grown-ups sometimes show they love each other in ways that kids don’t — and because adults are big and strong, their hugs and kisses look different than ones between kids or between parents and kids. Reassure your child that both Mommy and Daddy are just fine.

Despite not realizing that she interrupted sex, she may feel like she did something wrong if you shouted “Go away!” or “Get out!” when she walked in on you. Give yourself a break for reacting that way (she did startle you, after all) and then apologize for yelling. Remind her of a time when she was scared and reacted quickly — then tell her that moms and dads get surprised and act hastily too. After some comforting cuddles, distract her with an activity or a snack.

Moving forward, think about how you’ll prevent future sex interruptions. If your toddler's prone tonight waking, maybe you need a lock on your door (or a better one if your toddler burst past the one you have now). Maybe you need to check that your toddler’s crib or bed rails are secure so she can’t get out. Or maybe you just need to talk to your toddler about privacy: Make a game out of having her practice walking up to a closed door, knocking, and calling out “Mommy?” instead of opening the door. That’s a great skill that’ll prevent getting walked in on — and it may even let you change clothes or go to the bathroom without toddler input or interruption.

Last but not least, use this interrupted-sex incident as an opportunity to think about how you want to address sexuality issues with your little one. Becausechildren love asking questions, the day is coming (if it’s not already here)when she’ll ask how babies are made(or “What are those two doggies doing?”) — and you’ll want to be ready with an age-appropriate answer. Remember that her curiosity is normal, and it’s never too early to start simple conversations aboutbody partsand natural human impulses.

Wishing you interruption-free sex.
