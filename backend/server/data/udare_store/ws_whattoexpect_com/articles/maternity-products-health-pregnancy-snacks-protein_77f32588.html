Best High Protein Pregnancy Snacks

You’ve probably heard that it’s important to eat enough protein each day, especially because this macronutrient is thebuilding block of bones, muscles and skin. Because the amino acids contained within protein are essential to healthy cell growth, eating enough protein during pregnancy is important both forfetal developmentand the overall health of the pregnant person while the body is undergoing so much change. In addition to helping regulate growth hormones, blood sugar and cholesterol, a protein-rich diet may help prevent the development of gestational diabetes.

To make sure you’re meeting your nutritional needs during pregnancy, protein-packed snacks are a great way to get in those extra grams while keeping you satiated as baby grows. Here’s what you need to know about this key macronutrient, plus the best high-protein snacks to eat during pregnancy.

Other factors to consider

When selecting the best high-protein snacks to consume during pregnancy, you should also consider the following:

  • Portability: When looking for a good snack to fit your lifestyle, there are a few questions to ask yourself. Are you planning on relying on these snacks when you’re on the go? Can you throw them in your bag in case hunger strikes? You’ll want to consider how easily you can take these snacks with you and if they’ll survive a trip in your purse or backpack without making a mess (or getting crushed). Although it’s important to eat plenty of fresh, perishable foods, most of the foods on this list are non-perishable and don’t require a fridge, so they can be consumed in most situations.
  • Ingredients: It’s totally fine to eat some processed foods. Kapur acknowledges that we often have to rely on the convenience and shelf life of some packaged foods, and says there are a lot of pre-packaged foods that contain good nutrients. That being said, you should still check out the list of ingredients to make sure there’s nothing listed that you’re trying to avoid. Kapur recommends not eating too much sugar alcohol and looking for snacks that are mostly whole-foods based, like with nuts, seeds, fruit or eggs.

How much protein do I need during pregnancy?

Your protein intake should increase as you progress through your pregnancy, explains Kapur.

On average, Kapur recommends eating at least one gram of protein per kilogram of body weight, so for someone weighing 150 lbs, that’s about 68 grams of protein per day. However, protein needs will vary from person to person and depend on different factors like activity level and personal health history. If you’re concerned about your protein intake while pregnant, it’s important to speak with your doctor to discuss these various factors.

Kapur also recommends eating plenty of fiber along with your protein intake during pregnancy. “We think of [protein and fiber] like best friends; one enhances the function of the other,” she says. “So when you eat them together, you definitely have better blood sugar control, you have less of those offending symptoms that come with pregnancy like mood, low energy and heartburn.”

How can I fulfill my protein intake during pregnancy?

Although the amount of protein you need to eat each day during pregnancy will vary based on the factors listed above as well as how far along you are, aiming for about 70 to 75 grams a day at minimum is a good goal. This may look like 25 to 30 grams of protein per meal and another 10 to 20 grams per snack (give or take a few grams).

“The key with protein is to focus on variety and consistency more than just quantity,” Kapur says, adding that if you are getting protein from a variety of different sources and during most meals and snacks of the day, you’re likely to hit the amount your body needs.

This could be a mix of animal and plant foods, but if you’re someone who is following a plant-forward or vegan diet, you should be eating plenty of legumes, beans, lentils, whole grains and protein-packed veggies like green peas, spinach, asparagus and artichokes.

Although Kapur recommends pregnant people get their protein needs through food first, in some cases, you may want to supplement with something like a protein powder. Just be mindful of the ingredients. Look for protein powders, shakes and bars that have minimal ingredients and ideally are third party tested to look for any potential heavy metal contamination. These protein supplements should ideally have little to no added sugars and no sugar alcohols (which are sugar substitutes or sweeteners typically used in keto or low-sugar foods).

What high-protein foods are good for pregnancy nausea?

Pregnancy nausea can make it tough to meet your nutritional needs, especially during the first trimester. Although you don’t need extra calories in the first trimester like you do in the second and third, you should still be aiming to meet your protein requirements throughout pregnancy by eating a well-balanced diet.

Because pregnancy-related nausea can impact your appetite, some pregnant people find that cold foods are easier to take in than fragrant, warm foods. Some cold high-protein foods that might be easier to stomach include hard-boiled eggs, cold cooked chicken, quinoa, pasteurized Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, nut butters or protein shakes. Eating protein-rich foods in the morning may help with managing nausea throughout the day, too, says Kapur.

Ginger has been shown to relieve symptoms of nausea, so making a protein smoothie with fresh ginger is a good option. Bananas are rich in vitamin B6, which can also help with nausea, so Kapur recommends slicing a banana to eat with yogurt or nut butter. If you can stomach something warm, sipping on bone broth may also be easy to ingest while dealing with pregnancy nausea and getting in some protein and important nutrients like calcium, magnesium, zinc and B vitamins.
