10 Diwali Activities to Get Your Kids in on This Fun-Filled Celebration

diwali activities for kids, author akruti babaria and family dressed for diwali celebrations

Whether you're new to this holiday or grew up celebrating it, there are a lot of great ways to get the whole family excited about Diwali.

Every year, more than one billion people worldwide celebrate Diwali, the festival of lights that marks the victory of good over evil, light over darkness and knowledge over ignorance. Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists all celebrate this autumn holiday, which falls on November 1, 2024, per the lunar calendar. It's a time when families and friends pray together, eat yummy food, exchange sweets, decorate their homes and express gratitude.

There are many customs of Diwali that are fun and special to celebrate with kids. As an immigrant mom from India living in the U.S., it's especially important to me that my 2- and 7-year-olds learn about our family traditions. That said, it's often a lot harder to find a toddler-friendly craft kit for Diwali than, say, Halloween. The lack of authentic, kid-centered activities actually led me to start my own business,Kulture Khanza, which provides educational toys, puzzles, craft kits and other resources for cultural holidays.

My small business taught me a lot about how kids can learn about celebrations like Diwali and participate in the traditions. Here are 10 things you can do with your little ones this year that will both teach them something new and get them excited about this special holiday:

1. Create chalk art rangoli

chalk art rangoli

Rangoli is a traditional Indian floor art that's often made with colored sand, flowers or rice. You can create your own rangoli in your driveway, on the sidewalk or in the park using kid-friendlywashable chalk. Some popular shapes for rangoli include flowers, lotuses and paisleys. Draw an example to get your children started, and then let them see where their imagination takes them.

2. Fingerpaint rangoli

fingerpaint rangoli

Kids, especially toddlers, love getting messy. Print out afree rangoli templateand let them go at it withwashable finger paints(for easier clean-up). Thissensory activitywill help little ones learn about Diwali througharts and crafts.

3. Put together a rangoli puzzle

diwali rangoli puzzle

If bad weather is keeping you inside (or the idea of giving your little one sand, rice, paint or chalk just sounds messy), try making a rangoli in puzzle form. This 32-inchcircular floor puzzleis perfect for kids 3 and up and features 48 large, easy-to-handle pieces.

4. Craft a Diwali garland

diwali garland kids craft

Just like many other holidays, decorating the house is a big part of Diwali celebrations. Typically, families hang garlands on doorways as a sign of welcome and to invite positive energy. Try crafting this cute garland fromLove Laugh Mirchwith your kids. It showcases clay lamps called diyas, a symbol of Diwali that emphasizes the victory of light over darkness.

5. Read a book on Diwali

It's always a great idea toread aloud to your children, so let Diwali inspire your next library visit. For preschoolers, check outMy Diwali Lightby Raakhee Mirchani. This picture book tells the story of an Indian-American girl celebrating with family and friends. For babies and toddlers, tryDiwali (Celebrate The World)by Hannah Eliot. It's a beautiful board book that explains the five days of Diwali with vibrant illustrations.

6. Snack on a fruit diya

fruit diya kids diwali activity

Encourage yourpicky eaterto pick up some fruit by incorporating it into a Diwali-themed activity. Prepare some sliced cantaloupe — to represent a diya lamp — and strawberries (to stand in as the flame). You can also try using carrots, radishes, oranges, melons and mangoes if you'd like to serve up more produce options. Then encourage your kids to play with their food (and maybe take some bites).

7. Make a playdough diya

playdough diya kids diwali activity

If you've already gotplaydoughon hand, bring it out for this fun sensory activity. Create a three-dimensional diya by sculpting a bowl-shaped lamp and a small flame to go inside. You can even encourage preschoolers to add little gems on the lamp as decoration. Then display it as part of your Diwali home décor.

8. Dance to a Bollywood playlist

Little ones are always on the move. Use aBollywood dance playlistto get them grooving and moving (and working on thosegross motor skills). Pass out colorful scarves if you have them to amp up the excitement for this dance party.

9. Drink mango lassi together

mango lassi recipe

Even the pickiest of eaters will love sipping on a mango lassi — one of the most popular drinks at Indian restaurants. It's easy to make these sweet mango smoothies at home. (Try this simple recipe byZheelicious.) Omit cardamom if your kids aren't fans of the fragrant spice.

10. Watch 'Mira, Royal Detective'

If you're ready for some quiet time, queue up an educational show for your tot. Mira, Royal Detective is aDisney Plusseries about a girl named Mira who solves mysteries in a small Indian town. There's a Diwali-themed episode (The Great Diwali Mystery / The Case of the Curious Creature), but you don't need a subscription to show your kids the funDiwali song. This YouTube video will have you and your kids dancing along while listening about some key elements of the festival.
