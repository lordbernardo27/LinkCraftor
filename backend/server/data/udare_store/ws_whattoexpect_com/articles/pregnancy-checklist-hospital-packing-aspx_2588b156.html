You'll want a hospital bag packed and ready to head out the door with you when it's go-time. Here's what to put in it.

Peace of mind goes a long way when you're preparing for a baby. Getting your hospital bag organized ahead of time can help calm any jitters you may have about heading to the hospital or birthing center. You'll want to include items for you, your baby, and your partner or coach (who will likely have some down time).

As for what to bring? Our helpful hospital bag checklist can be your guide.

Key Takeaways

  • Packing your hospital bag ahead of time can ease stress before labor. It's a good idea to get your bag ready a few weeks before your due date and keep it near the door or in your car, so you’re prepared whenever labor starts.
  • Focus on comfort and essentials for yourself, your baby, and your support person. Bring important documents, toiletries, comfortable clothes, chargers, and postpartum items for yourself; a simple going-home outfit and blanket for baby; and snacks, toiletries, and extra clothes for your partner or coach.
  • You don’t need to overpack because the hospital provides many basics. Hospitals typically supply items like diapers, mesh underwear, pads, and gowns. Skip valuables, excess clothing, and unnecessary extras to keep your bag manageable.

What should I pack for myself?

Keep in mind that the hospital will likely provide some basics, including disposable mesh underwear, grip socks, a gown to wear during and after labor, extra-thick sanitary pads, and a few toiletries (soap and a toothbrush). Still, you might prefer your own versions of these items or extras, just in case.

“It’s nice to pack your own toiletries and any other items that may make your labor, delivery, and postpartum time more comfortable,” notesDr. Jennifer Butt, an OB/GYN atUpper East Side OB/GYNin New York City and a member of theWhat to Expect Medical Review Board.

What should I pack for my baby?

Your baby won’t need much, but here are a few items to consider:

  • Going-home outfit, including socks or booties
  • Weather-appropriate accessories, such as a knit cap if it’s cold out, or a hat with a little brim if it's sunny and warm.
  • A blanket

While it won't fit it in your hospital bag, you'll want to have your car seat ready to go. In fact, a few weeks before your due date, go ahead and have it installed and inspected by a certified technician. You can find one through the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. What should I pack for my partner or coach?

Labor can be long — and there will be times when your partner or coach doesn't have much to do. Here’s what might come in handy:

  • Phone and a charger
  • Gum, mints, lip balm
  • Toothbrush, toothpaste, deodorant, other toiletries
  • Spare contact lenses, glasses
  • A travel pillow or bed pillow and a blanket
  • Sweatshirt or jacket for quick runs to the drugstore or deli
  • A change of underwear and fresh shirt in case labor goes on — and on
  • Pajamas or sweats in case of an overnight stay
  • Plenty of snacks, especially ones that keep well (pretzels, trail mix, granola bars)
  • Small bills or change for vending machines and the hospital cafeteria
  • Reusable water bottle or sports drinks
  • A camera or video camera, if desired
  • Books, newspaper, magazines, orgamesfor downtime

Is there anything I shouldn’t bring to the hospital?

You can certainly overdo it when it comes to packing your hospital bag. Here’s what to leave at home:

  • Too much clothing for you or the baby . Choose one outfit each and leave the rest at home.
  • Valuables . Don’t bring diamond earrings, the priceless watch your dad gave you, or anything you’d be sad to lose. Hospitals aren’t hot beds of crime, but better safe than sorry.
  • Large amounts of cash . Rely on your partner, coach, or relatives with you in the hospital to pay for food, drinks, and snacks.
  • Diapers . The hospital will provide enough during your stay.

TheAmerican College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists(ACOG) recommends keeping your packed hospital bag near the front door or in your car trunk once it's ready.

And don’t forget Fluffy! Arrange for a friend or family member to pop in and check that your pet(s) are taken care of while you're gone.
