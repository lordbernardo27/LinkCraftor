Hemorrhoids are common during pregnancy due to increased pelvic pressure, blood flow, and constipation. While they often go away after delivery, they can also show up after birth, thanks to the pushing you did during labor. Hemorrhoids can be uncomfortable but are usually manageable with simple steps like staying hydrated, eating fiber, avoiding straining, and using soothing remedies.

Key Takeaways

  • Hemorrhoids are common during pregnancy. More than half of pregnant women experience them, typically in the second or third trimester, due to increased pelvic pressure, blood flow, and constipation.
  • Constipation and straining are major triggers. Hormonal changes, a growing uterus, and hard stools can all contribute to swollen rectal veins — and pushing during labor can make hemorrhoids appear or worsen postpartum.
  • Simple habits can bring real relief. Staying hydrated, eating more fiber, moving regularly, avoiding straining, and using soothing remedies (like warm baths or witch hazel pads) can help ease discomfort — but check with your health care practitioner if you notice any bleeding.

What are hemorrhoids?

Hemorrhoids arevaricose veinsin the rectum. They're also known as piles because of the resemblance these swollen veins sometimes bear to a pile of grapes or marbles (now you know why they're no fun to sit on).

Hemorrhoids can be itchy, uncomfortable, and downright painful.

When do hemorrhoids start during pregnancy?

Hemorrhoids are especially common in the mid-second trimester to third trimester of pregnancy, although they can appear at any time. About 30 to 40% of pregnant women deal with hemorrhoids.

What causes hemorrhoids during pregnancy?

Pressure from your enlarging uterus starting aroundweek 25, plus increased blood flow to the pelvic area, can cause the veins in the rectal wall to swell, bulge, and itch.

Constipation can aggravate, or even cause, hemorrhoids. How can I get some relief from hemorrhoids while I'm pregnant?

Hemorrhoids are no fun, but there are plenty of things you can do to minimize and treat them. Here are some tips:

  • Stay regular. The best hemorrhoid treatment during pregnancy is to stay regular, so drink plenty of water , and up your fiber intake to avoid constipation . Trusted SourceMayo ClinicWhat Can I Do About Hemorrhoids During Pregnancy?See All Sources [2]
  • Do your Kegel exercises. In addition to preparing your perineal floor for birth,Kegel exercisescan help prevent hemorrhoids by improving circulation to the area.
  • Sleep on your side. Avoid snoozing on your back, which you shouldn't be doing anyway after yourfirst trimester. This reduces pressure on the affected area. You can also try lying down on your left side a few times a day to relieve the pressure on your rectal veins.
  • Keep moving. Don't sit or stand for long stretches at a time. Try taking a brisk five-minute walk every hour or so to improve circulation to the area and flush things out. And if you have your doctor's okay, keep upsafe pregnancy exercisesright until yourdue date.
  • Don't force it. Don't strain or linger on the toilet.
  • Stay clean as a whistle. Use warm water and white two-ply toilet paper to wipe after bowel movements. Don't wipe too hard, either, which can irritate sensitive tissues. Try wipes if toilet paper is too harsh for your sensitive backside.
  • Take a warm bath. A 10- to 15-minute soak in the tub will help you stay clean and may help reduce discomfort. "Sitting in a bath with Epsom salt is what really helps," says Community mom michigandreamer. "I did that multiple times a day."
  • Try witch hazel pads, wipes, or ice packs. These can help soothe the sting ofhemorrhoids. "I take a jar and fill it halfway with witch hazel liquid and the other half with water. Pop it in the fridge, soak a cotton ball or two, then place them on your hemorrhoids for about 10 minutes," suggests Community mom Kiwi1025. "It can be pretty relieving."
  • Get a donut-shaped pillow. If sitting is really uncomfortable, it can ease the pressure. "I had a donut pillow postpartum and it helped relieve pain from hemorrhoids while sitting," says Community mom jellyfish55.
  • Talk to your practitioner about hemorrhoid treatments. Your practitioner may recommend a stool softener or topical cream or ointment to relieve the itching and pain. You can purchase many of these over the counter.
