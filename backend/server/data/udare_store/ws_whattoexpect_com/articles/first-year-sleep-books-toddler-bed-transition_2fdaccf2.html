7 Books to Help Your Toddler Transition to Their Big Kid Bed

toddler bed transition

Transitioning your child from a crib to a toddler bed is a big deal — for everyone. What to Expect mom Mere559 is currently struggling with the transition right now.

"Since baby brother is coming we decided to go ahead and put our daughter in a big girl bed so she can hopefully be used to it before he gets here," says Mere559. "Last night was her first night in the new bed and she got up four or five times and needed us to lay with her each time. I know it’s not going to be perfect right away but I wanted to see if anyone had any advice so we don’t start forming any bad habits that will be harder to break later on."

If you’re having similar questions (and concerns), you’re not alone. “Toddlers often struggle with the crib-to-bed transition because it represents a major developmental shift,” explainsFrancyne Zeltser, Psy.D., a psychologist who practices in New York City.

“A crib provides a contained, predictable sleep space that feels safe and familiar. Moving to a bed introduces greater freedom and independence, which many toddlers are not yet developmentally prepared to manage," she says. "This can trigger anxiety about the unfamiliar setup, bedtime resistance, difficulty separating at bedtime, or wandering/calling for parents during the night.”

Parents typically update their little one’s sleep environment any timebetween 18 months and 3 years, but ideally closer to age 3.

So how can you prepare your toddler for the big change? Many parents have found comfort in books explaining this next step, finding that sharing stories of other kids or beloved characters like Elmo, make this change a little easier. Below, review the books recommended by parents and experts:

Big Enough for a Bed (Sesame Street)

Why We Love It

Elmo has outgrown his crib, and the big question looms: What’s next? While it may take some time, his favorite blanket and teddy bear help bring similar elements from his crib to an otherwise uncharted territory. By the end of the book, he feels comfy and cozy in his new big kid bed.

“This book uses a familiar, comforting character (Elmo) to model the transition in a calm, gradual way,” Dr. Zeltser says. “Toddlers relate easily to Elmo’s feelings and routines, making the story a helpful tool for normalizing initial discomfort and showing that the new bed can become a cozy, safe space.”

My Big Bed by Little Hippo Books

Why We Love It

Little Giraffe has a sibling coming soon, and he’s feeling scared about the prospect of sleeping in a new bed. This imaginative board book comes with touch-and-feel elements for a sensory experience. By the end, Little Giraffe realizes his new bed isn’t so scary, and is happy to give his crib to the newest member of the family.

The sensory parts of the book align well with toddlers’ developmental needs, says Zeltser. “Its tactile pages and simple storyline help reduce anxiety by making the new bed feel fun rather than intimidating, which supports the idea that the transition is a natural part of growing up.”

In My Own Bed by Lovevery

Why We Love It

One of several books in Lovevery’s Tricky Topics collection, In My Own Bed covers the crib-to-bed transition with real images and an easy-to-follow story showing dialogue between the main character Kepa and his parents. Kepa wonders why this is happening, and his dad explains that he needs more room to accommodate his growing body. They go through his typical nighttime routine, with one swap: Kepa’s new bed. To make the process even easier, you can access more resources through the book’s QR code.

Big Kid Bed by Leslie Patricelli

Why We Love It

Even though Baby is excited about some aspects of the new bed, it’s clear there are new challenges and fears ahead. Baby notes that compared to the crib, the bed is “very, very tall.” “What if I fall?” he wonders. One book in a series, parents and kids alike rave about how it shows the ups and downs of this new sleep space.

A Big Kid Bed is Coming by Liz Fletcher

Why We Love It

Louie is a cap-wearing super elephant, with a new adventure ahead: his big kid bed. He’s trepidacious at first, but eventually decides to give it a try. He surprises himself by seeing how brave he can be (and how well he can sleep in his new bed!).

“With its rhyming structure and bravery-building theme, this book helps toddlers understand that nervousness is part of learning something new,” Dr. Zeltser says. “Louie’s journey models courage and perseverance, supporting toddlers who may feel hesitant about the change.”

Big Bed for Giraffe by Michael Dahl

Why We Love It

Another character named Little Giraffe can’t fit into his crib anymore. His toys no longer fit inside, and he even tries to climb out (one part of the book some parents could do without!). It’s not easy at the beginning, but he soon learns how comfortable it is to sleep in a bed. He tries out a few toddler bed options and finally finds his perfect match.

“This story introduces problem-solving around the challenges of moving to a bed, like toys fitting differently or feeling physically uncertain,” Dr. Zeltser says. “It mirrors the trial-and-error toddlers experience during this transition and reassures them that they will eventually find comfort in their new sleep space.”

Your Own Big Bed by Rita M. Bergstein

Why We Love It

Your toddler isn’t the only one growing. In this book, a little boy sees how a chick, an alligator, and a sea turtle are all getting bigger, too. From taking their first steps to settling into a larger sleeping area, there are a lot of changes for every person and animal throughout childhood.

“By paralleling the child’s development with the growth of animals, this book reinforces that transitioning to a bigger sleep space is a natural part of getting older,” Dr. Zeltser says. “The narrative gently encourages autonomy and pride, which is helpful for toddlers who feel unsure about leaving the familiarity of the crib.”
