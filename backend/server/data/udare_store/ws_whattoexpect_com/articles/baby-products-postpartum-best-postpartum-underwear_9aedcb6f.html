A Doctor's Note on Postpartum Bleeding

Let’s talk about something every new mom goes through but few are prepared for:postpartum bleeding, or lochia. Whether you had a vaginal birth or a C-section, your body will shed the extra tissue from pregnancy.

Bleeding is typically heaviest in the first week postpartum, or the first stage of lochia, before gradually lightening over time. During the second stage of lochia, you can expect pink or brown blood for roughly 12 days. In the final stage of lochia, you'll likely see spotting or a yellowish discharge for up to six weeks.

During this time, postpartum underwear is your first line of defense. These super-comfy panties are roomy enough to hold maxi pads or ice packs for soreness; you shouldn't use tampons or menstrual cups until your doctor gives you approval, as there is a heightenedrisk of infectionduring this time.

FAQs

What type of underwear is best after giving birth?

The best postpartum underwear for you will depend on personal preference. For instance, do you prefer a compression panel? How do you feel about the environmental impact of disposables?

It will also depend on how you gave birth. New moms who had a C-section usually prefer a higher waistband, for example.

From disposable underwear to underwear with built-in silicone pads to cover a cesarean incision, the best type of postpartum underwear will vary from person to person.

How many pairs should I buy for early postpartum?

This depends on a number of factors, including the type of postpartum underwear you’re thinking of getting. Many women find that they only need to use disposable underwear for the first week or so after giving birth, after which point they switch to regular underwear with a pad. So if you’re going the disposable route, you may find that you just need enough for a few weeks. (Assume you’ll be changing them often in the first few days.)

The quantity of non-disposables you need just depends on how frequently you want to do laundry. Even with a pad, it’s possible to experience leaks in the early days, so having a few more pairs than you think you need is always a good idea.

Are disposable underwear good for postpartum?

Immediately after delivery, you'll likely wear the disposable undies you're given in the hospital. In fact, docs often suggest stashing a bunch of these to take home, if you can.

If you prefer disposable mesh underwear, know that you can also buy more pairs to use at home in those first few weeks. Disposable postpartum underwear means less laundry, but it also means buying more packs.

Can I wear normal underwear after a C-section?

If you’re a C-section mom, you should avoid bikini briefs (or anything that hits low) for several weeks, since most people have a surgical incision right above where their pubic hairline starts. High-waisted underwear is usually what those who had a C-section opt for, as well as underwear that has some compression or support to hold you in. There are also some pairs of postpartum underwear that have a silicone band designed to go over your incision to help prevent it from getting irritated.

How long do you need to wear postpartum underwear?

This can vary a lot from mom to mom, but most women experience their heaviestlochia, or postpartum bleeding, for the first 10 days after giving birth. After that, lochia usually transitions into lighter bleeding or spotting for four to six weeks. Postpartum underwear will probably come in handy the most during these first few weeks and months. But many moms find postpartum undies to be especially comfortable and continue wearing them beyond that point (in fact, some of the pairs on this list have rave reviews for working as “regular” underwear, too).

What size postpartum underwear should I get?

When shopping for postpartum underwear, you may want to order a size larger than you normally would to accommodate your postpartum body and have room for pads, particularly if the underwear is a non-postpartum style. If you’re buying underwear specifically marketed for maternity or the postpartum period, it might be safe to go with your regular size — just check the brand’s sizing chart to be sure.

Think of postpartum underwear like burp cloths — you’ll want more than you think. My advice is to have enough to keep on rotation without having to stress about doing laundry on the daily. I consider 6 to 10 pairs a general sweet spot, though it'll depend on your personal needs. As far as disposable underwear goes, make sure you have enough on hand for the first week.

What styles work best with maxi pads or ice packs?

High-waisted, full-coverage pairs of underwear are best. However, if you've had a C-section, be mindful of where the band sits on your incision.

How do I wash postpartum underwear safely?

It's always best to check with the brand's care instructions. In general, to keep your underwear clean and extend its lifespan, I recommend washing these on a gentle cycle with a mild detergent. Line dry when you can, otherwise, opt for low heat.
