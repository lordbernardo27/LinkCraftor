Yes, Your Feet Really Are Getting Bigger: Why 75% of Pregnant Women Experience Swelling

Updates to copy, information, sources, and formatting

swollen feet during pregnancy

Swelling in your feet and ankles is a common part of pregnancy — but how much is normal? Learn what causes edema, how to find relief, and when it might be worth a call to your provider.

Have you jumped a shoe size? Swelling during pregnancy — especially in your ankles and feet — is common and expected. Extra body fluid builds up, and your growing uterus presses on pelvic veins and the vena cava, slowing circulation. To top it off, the hormone relaxin loosens ligaments, allowing your feet to spread.

Read on to learn what causes pregnancy swelling (also called edema), when it might signal a problem (not usually), and how to cope.

Key Takeaways

  • Swelling is common — and usually normal. About 75% of pregnant women experience swelling (aka edema), often starting in the second trimester and lasting until delivery. It's caused by increased fluids and pressure from your growing uterus, and it tends to worsen later in the day or in warm weather.
  • Simple habits can help reduce discomfort. Elevating your feet, sleeping on your side (especially the left), staying active, wearing compression socks and supportive footwear, and drinking plenty of water can all help minimize swelling and keep you more comfortable.
  • Know when swelling could signal a problem. While mild swelling is harmless, sudden or severe swelling — especially in the face, one leg, or alongside symptoms like rapid weight gain or pain — should be checked by your provider, as it can be a sign of something more serious.

It's also normal not to experience noticeable swelling. While edema affects about three-quarters of pregnant women, that means one in four lucky pregnant women don't.

What causes swollen ankles and feet during pregnancy?

Edema is the result of increased body fluids, including higher blood volume. In addition, your growing uterus puts pressure on your pelvic veins and your vena cava (the large vein on the right side of your body that returns blood from your lower limbs to your heart).

During pregnancy, body fluids increase to nurture both you and your baby, and these fluids can accumulate in your tissues. In fact, your body's water content increases by about 6.5 liters (up to 15 pounds) by term. This fluid supports your growing baby, placenta, amniotic fluid, and your own blood volume.

The amount of swelling you experience can vary by the hour (often increasing in the evening) and by the weather (with warmer temperatures leading to more swelling). You may also notice swelling in your hands as well as your ankles and feet.

"I had to take my wedding ring off a few weeks ago," saysWhat to Expect Communitymom youraveragegirl. "I almost couldn't get it off my finger, which has apparently started to swell."

In addition to edema, another factor may be at play if your shoes feel snug: Like the rest of your ligaments, those in your feet loosen thanks to thehormone relaxin, allowing the bones to spread.

Don't depend on your feet returning to pre-pregnancy size immediately after birth. While swelling will recede and ligaments will tighten, your feet may remain permanently larger — by up to a full shoe size.

Are there risks associated with swollen ankles and feet?

Mild swelling of the ankles and feet is usually harmless and perfectly normal, but sometimes swelling can be a red flag. Call your practitioner right away if you notice:

  • Excessive swelling
  • A puffy face
  • Gaining more than five pounds in one week
  • Swelling that doesn't improve overnight
  • One leg that's swollen or feels heavy
  • Pain in one calf when flexing your foot
  • Skin on one leg that's red or warm to the touch

Excessive swelling can be one sign ofpreeclampsia— but when it is, it's accompanied by other symptoms (such aselevated blood pressure, protein in the urine, and possibly rapid weight gain). If your blood pressure and urine checks are normal (these are routinely checked atprenatal visits), preeclampsia is unlikely to be a concern.

Rarely, leg swelling can signal adeep vein thrombosis(DVT), a potentially life-threatening condition caused by a blood clot in a deep vein. Swelling from DVT typically affects only one leg (usually the left).

This asymmetrical swelling is significant — the affected calf may be more than 1 centimeter larger than the unaffected leg. If you notice any of these signs, call your practitioner right away. Your doctor can evaluate you with a noninvasive ultrasound of the veins in your legs.

How to reduce swelling while pregnant

Some edema might be unavoidable, but there are things you can do to minimize the swelling and feel more comfortable:

  • Avoid long periods of standing or sitting. If you're on your feet a lot, take breaks to sit. Trusted SourceKidsHealth From NemoursDealing With Swollen Ankles During PregnancySee All Sources [1] If you're sitting for long stretches, take a 5-minute stroll at least once an hour.
  • Kick up your feet. If possible, elevate your legs when you're sitting. Who has a better excuse to put up her feet than a pregnant woman? "When it's too bad, I lay on my bed with my feet on the headboard," says Community mom Dahlquah.
  • Sleep on your side. If you don't already, try sleeping on your side (preferably your left). This can help keep your kidneys humming along, which helps eliminate waste and reduce swelling.
  • Stay active. Gentle exercises likewalkingorswimmingcan improve circulation and reduce fluid buildup. "I've discovered swimming helps with my swelling," says Community mom amycrystal3.
  • Avoid tight socks. Avoid anything that restricts circulation around your calves or ankles. Socks that leave an indentation mark around your leg are likely too tight.
  • Wear comfy shoes. Consider orthotic shoes or inserts, which can make your feet feel better and can reduce leg andback pain during pregnancy, too. (Those sexy slingbacks may not fit now, anyway.) Once you get home, switch to a pair of soft slippers. "I had to buy some shoes in a wide size because I had a lot of swelling," says Community mom slsxoxo.
  • Try compression socks or stockings. Opt for full pantyhose (with extra tummy room) orknee- or thigh-highs(a better choice if you're perpetually warm) that aren't tight on top. Whichever type you choose, put them on in the morning before the swelling starts so they're more effective. "My swelling can get really bad in my legs, especially when it's warm and/or I'm on my feet a lot, and compression socks help tremendously," says Community member Amingerink.
  • Drink lots of water. It may seem counterintuitive to try to flush out fluids with fluids, butdrinking 8 to 10 glasses of watera day will help flush excess sodium and waste.
  • Watch your salt intake. Eating too much salty food can increase swelling. Trusted SourceMayo ClinicEdemaSee All Sources [2] You don't have to give up salting your food, just go easy.

While swelling during pregnancy can be uncomfortable, it's usually a normal part of your body's incredible work supporting your baby. Pay attention to your symptoms, take simple steps to stay comfortable, and don't hesitate to check in with your provider if something feels off.
