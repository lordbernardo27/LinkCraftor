Nearly 90,000 Bottles of Children's Ibuprofen Recalled for Foreign Substance

Children's ibuprofen recall, liquid medication being poured

Here's what to look for in your medicine cabinet.

A nationwide recall has been issued for 89,592 bottles of children's ibuprofen after reports of a foreign substance in the medication, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) says.

Strides Pharma, the medication's manufacturer, issued the recall after it "received complaints for a gel-like mass and black particles in the product," according to theFDA enforcement report.

The medication was made in India and manufactured for Taro Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc., and sold across the United States.

What to Expect reached out to Strides Pharma and Taro Pharmaceuticals representatives for comment. We will update this story if we receive a response.

Key Takeaways

  • Nearly 90,000 bottles of liquid children's ibuprofen have been recalled nationwide because of a foreign substance in the medication.
  • The recalled bottles have lot numbers of 7261973A and 7261974A, and expiration dates of Jan. 31, 2027.
  • If you have a recalled bottle, stop using the medication and call your health care provider if you think you gave your child some of the recalled medication.

Which bottles of children's ibuprofen are recalled?

The recall is for liquid children's ibuprofen that comes in bottles of 4 fluid ounces (120 milliliters). You may have a recalled bottle if the label says the following:

  • It has a lot number of 7261973A or 7261974A.
  • It has an expiration date of Jan. 31, 2027.
  • It was manufactured for Taro Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc., Hawthorne, NY 10532.
  • It was made in India.

What should you do if you have a recalled bottle?

To be on the safe side, avoid giving your child the recalled medication unless necessary. If you think you gave your child some of the recalled medication, call your health care provider.

The FDA issued aClass II recallfor the ibuprofen, which means using it "may cause temporary health consequences, but the probability of a serious health issue is remote." The FDA says it's generally safe to continue using a medication under a Class II recall, but check with your provider first.

And alwayscheck with your pediatricianbefore giving any medications to a child under 2 years old.
