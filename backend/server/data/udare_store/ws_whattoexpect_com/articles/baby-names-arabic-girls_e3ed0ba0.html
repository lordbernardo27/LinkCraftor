20 Beautiful Arabic Baby Girl Names

Minor copy updates.

baby girl with Arabic name

Looking for a beautiful, unique, and meaningful name for your little lady? Take some inspiration from these popular Arabic baby girl names.

Deciding on a name for your baby girl can be both exciting and overwhelming: After all, it is one of the earliest and most influential decisions you make in your baby’s life.

If your name selection criteria include a feminine and beautiful-sounding name that will make your daughter stand out from the crowd, consider this list of trendy and popular Arabic baby girl names. (And if you're not sure whether you're having a boy or a girl, check out our favoriteArabic baby boy names, too.)

1. Alia. A prestigious name for your esteemed little girl, Alia, pronounced al-ya, literally translates to “high.” It’s used to describe an honorable woman or a girl who holds a high social standing and commands the respect and appreciation of everyone around her. Alternate spellings include Alya and Aliaa.

2. Amira. Meaning “princess,” this lofty name has always been popular among Arab-speaking families. Miro is one of many adorable nicknames you could call your little lady.

3. Aysha. This name comes from the Arabic word "aiysh," meaning “life.” Aysha means “full of life, vibrant, alive, and well.” Many families choose this timeless name as a token of a long and lucky life.

4. Dima. Used in both Arabic and Russian, this dainty name has similar meanings in both languages. In Russian, it means “strong fighter,” while in Arabic, it means “continuous, enduring, and everlasting.” It can also translate as a pleasant rain without thunder or lightning.

5. Farida. Often associated with sophistication and social status, this elegant name was held by Queen Farida of Egypt in the 40s. Pronounced fa-reeda, it literally translates to “unique” — a lovely salute to your one-of-a-kind gal.

6. Farrah. This gorgeous baby girl name, pronounced far-rah, means “joy, happiness” and is a timeless, go-to choice among Arabic families. What more perfect way to celebrate your little bundle of joy?

7. Jamila. There’s no wonder why this name remains popular in almost all Arab-speaking countries: It means “beautiful.” Derived from the Arabic noun "jamal" (which has the same meaning), Jamila describes beauty in both looks and character. You might just call your cutie Gigi for short.

8. Layan. Its meaning is as delicate and sweet as it sounds: “soft, delicate, and tender.” A widespread name in many Arab countries — especially in Lebanon, Jordan and Syria — you could also call your sweetie by the common nickname Lily.

9. Mariam. Also spelled (and pronounced) Maryem, Mariam is the Arabic form of the biblical name Miriam. While the name’s origins can be traced back to many languages and religious references, it refers to a woman who is admired for her devotion and helpfulness — characteristics we all hope for in our little ones.

10. Miral. Originating from Turkey, this name means “small gazelle.” Despite its Turkish origins, this charming name is gaining popularity in the Arab world. You can call your baby Mira for short.

11. Nadia. Traditional yet trendy, Nadia is derived from the word "nada," meaning “morning dew” and is used to describe a generous and a natural giver. Such a light and pretty name for your baby girl!

12. Nadine. Here’s a sweet cross-cultural choice: Nadine’s origin can be traced back to several languages, including French, Greek, and Russian, where it means “hope.” In the Arabic language, this popular name translates to “bearer of good news, messenger of blessings.”

13. Nour. You simply can’t go wrong with this name, which means “light” or “luminescence.” It’s timeless, gender-neutral, and short and sweet.

14. Rayya. This baby girl name, pronounced rye-ya, sounds lovely — and has an even lovelier meaning. While Rayya literally translates to “satisfied thirst,” “green leaves,” or “fragrant breeze,” it refers to a woman who lives a life of prosperity, with all her needs quenched.

15. Salma. Common in both the Arab world and in Spain, this exquisite name means “safe, well, and sound.” It comes from the Arabic word "salam," meaning “peace.”

16. Sofana. One of the most authentic Arabic names, Sofana is derived from the word "sawfa," meaning “will happen.” The name itself means “patient, calm, tolerant, and willing to endure.” Sofy would be a cute nickname.

17. Tala. This baby name’s popularity has been on the rise across Arab countries for the past couple of years — not only because it’s easy to pronounce and write, but also because it means a “beautiful palm tree” in Arabic and a “beautiful and youthful lady” in Greek.

18. Tia. Despite its popularity in many Arab countries in recent years, the origin of Tia, pronounced tee-ya, can be tracked to several languages. In Greek, it means “princess,” and in Spanish it means “aunt.” In Arabic, Tia refers to delicacy and femininity.

19. Yasmeen. Props to you if you already guessed it: Yasmeen means “jasmine flower.” This sweet Arabic name withstands the test of time, remaining a favorite baby girl name throughout Arabic countries.

20. Zainah. A popular Arabic name that means “pretty, lovely, or gorgeous,” zainah can also mean “decoration.” As a name, Zainah (pronounced zane-nah) refers to a girl or a woman who is “decorated” by her graciousness and good manners.
