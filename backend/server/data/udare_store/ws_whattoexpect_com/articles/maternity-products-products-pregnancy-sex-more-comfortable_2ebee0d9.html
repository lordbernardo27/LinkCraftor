Best Products to Make Pregnancy Sex More Comfortable

There’s one thing that’s often true aboutpregnancy sex: It’s not always comfortable. You’re dealing with a whole range of pregnancy symptoms (includingmorning sickness,back pain,constipation,heartburnandgas) that make simple, everyday activities difficult, let alone being physically intimate with another person. And as the weeks go on, you’re also managing an expanding baby bump that can make moving around more challenging.

“As the uterus grows, finding a comfortable position for sex can be a bit of a challenge,” explainsKameelah Phillips, M.D., OB/GYN in New York City and member of the What to Expect Medical Review Board. “The weight of the uterus can cause pelvic pressure and limit an expecting mother’s mobility.”

Penetration itself might also be more tricky. The cervix softens during pregnancy and could be more sensitive, so penetration from a penis or toy that is too deep or forceful could be uncomfortable, saysStaci Tanouye, M.D., an OB/GYN in private practice in Jacksonville, Florida.

Despite the physical challenges of pregnancy sex, there are plenty of ways to find relief and alleviate discomfort. With items like pregnancy pillows to alleviate pressure andsex toysyou can use externally to avoid penetration, you can find a way to make pregnancy sex comfortable — and highly enjoyable — for your changing body.

Other factors to consider

When shopping for products to use during pregnancy sex, there are a few other qualities to consider:

  • Supportiveness: It’s important to have items that provide physical comfortable by supporting you in different positions. Pillows or wedges can be used to support your knees, hips and lower back. Keep in mind that your body will continue to change throughout the trimesters and your go-to positions may vary, so you need pillows that can be versatile and provide help propping you up and keeping you comfy. Another key factor is the support of something like a bra. Your breast size will likely shift throughout pregnancy as you get closer to delivery, and something soft and comfortable that gives you a bit of a lift might help you feel best in your own skin.
  • Ingredients and materials: Anything you’re applying in or on your body while you’re pregnant should contain pregnancy-safe ingredients, while avoiding fragrances or chemical additives. When shopping for lubes to enhance your pleasure and cut back on friction that could irritate you during sex, Dr. Tanouye says to avoid ones that are scented or have “warming” or “cooling” properties. Because your vulva, vagina and cervix might be extra sensitive during pregnancy, any products with a scent or temperature-altering properties can intensify irritation. As far as materials go, Dr. Tanouye suggests using sex toys that are made of non-porous material. This way, you can easily clean them and avoid areas that may harbor infection-causing bacteria. Hormones may also make yourskin sensitive during pregnancy, so you may want to look for pregnancy pillows with cases made of cotton that will be gentle on your skin.
  • Adjustability: When shopping for sexual wellness products, the ability to adjust based on your current size or comfort level is a plus. For example, a sex toy that has many different modes to switch between to find the right intensity level for your sensitive vulva and clitoris would be a win because you can adjust as needed throughout your pregnancy (and beyond). A pregnancy pillow that you can configure in multiple different positions would also be a great investment.

It’s worth noting that most toys and sex products that you used before getting pregnant should still be safe to use, as long as you feel comfortable using them and your doctor has confirmed that you have a low-risk pregnancy, according to Dr. Tanouye. Those experiencing a high-risk pregnancy or pregnancy complications, however, should check with their doctor before testing out a new toy or type of stimulation to make sure it’s safe to use.

Does sex feel different when pregnant?

“Sex during pregnancy can be both more pleasurable, due to increased circulation in the vulva and vagina, as well as more sensitive for the same reasons,” says Dr. Tanouye.

As we already mentioned, it can be harder to get comfortable during sex while pregnant because of your growing belly and pain spots like a sore back or tender breasts. And, a more sensitive cervix could mean having to be gentler with penetrative sex or sex with toys.

Yet, despite some potential hurdles to overcome, many pregnant people experience increased sexual desire. In fact, you may even have a better climax during pregnancy. “Many pregnant people report more intense orgasms due to increased blood circulation to the genitals,” Dr. Tanouye notes.

How can I get comfortable during sex while pregnant?

  • Try body oils, memory foam pillows and water-based lubricants. These products can help reduce discomfort during intimate sessions with another person or solo. Lubricants can help with vaginal dryness, a common symptom during pregnancy, while body oils are not only great for getting in the mood, but they can also help with dryness. Pregnancy pillows can be used to provide extra support during sex, especially if you’re in a position that might put pressure on a tender spot like your lower back.
  • Alter your penetration depth if needed. Because your cervix is softer during pregnancy, those who have penetrative sex may need to swap out some positions that involve deeper penetration (like doggy style) for something that involves getting on top of a partner. Dr. Tanouye adds that a ring or bumper can help prevent pain due to depth of penetration.
  • Use sex toys. Vibrators, dildos and massage wands can especially be helpful in later months when you may not be able to move as much during sex. If penetrative sex is uncomfortable for you, look for toys that you can use externally or internal sex toys in which you can control the depth of penetration.
  • Check in to see how your body feels. If you’re experiencing pain that has you concerned or vaginal bleeding during sex, check in with your doctor. These symptoms can be signs of more serious issues or may require expert advice to find a way to have sex more comfortably while you’re expecting.
