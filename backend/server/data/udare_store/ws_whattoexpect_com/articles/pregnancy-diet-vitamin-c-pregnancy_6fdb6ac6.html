Vitamin C during pregnancy

Vitamin C is important for your baby's development — and you can use the immunity boost. Find out how much vitamin C you need each day during pregnancy and the best way to get it.

You need 85 milligrams of vitamin C daily while pregnant — and if you're eating a healthy diet that includes plenty of vitamin-C-rich foods, you're probably already getting enough. Still, it's important to make sure you meet your daily recommended intake. Vitamin C plays a key role in your baby's development and supports your immune system.

Here's your complete guide tovitamin Cduring pregnancy, including why it's important, how much you need, and the C-rich foods that can help you get enough.

Key Takeaways

  • Vitamin C does more than support immunity during pregnancy . It also helps with tissue repair, collagen production, iron absorption, and your baby's bone and tooth development.
  • Most pregnant women can meet their daily vitamin C needs (85 mg) through a healthy diet and prenatal vitamin . A diet that includes fruits and vegetables like oranges, strawberries, bell peppers, and broccoli is usually sufficient.
  • Extra vitamin C supplements usually aren't recommended during pregnancy . Experts generally suggest focusing on food sources instead. Talk with your health care practitioner if you think you're not getting enough.

Why is vitamin C important during pregnancy?

During pregnancy, vitamin C supports:

  • Yourbaby's bone development
  • Your baby's tooth development
  • Tissue repair and wound healing
  • Your body's production of collagen
  • Immunity
  • Ironabsorption

Some studies link low blood levels of vitamin C during pregnancy topreeclampsia, which is why it's especially important to get enough when you're expecting.

Because vitamin C is a water-soluble vitamin, your human body can't store it. That means you need a fresh supply from your diet every day.

How much vitamin C do pregnant women need?

Pregnant women ages 19 and older should aim for about 85 milligrams (mg) of vitamin C daily.

To put this into perspective, a 6-ounce glass of orange juice plus 1 cup of strawberries will deliver more than double your daily dose of vitamin C. Aim to include about three servings of vitamin C-rich fruits and veggies per day, and you'll likely meet your goal.

What are the best vitamin C-rich foods for pregnant women?

When you hear "vitamin C," you likely think of oranges — but citrus isn't your only option. Plenty of fruits and vegetables are rich in vitamin C. Keep in mind that vitamin C breaks down with heat, so try to eat some vitamin-C rich foods raw each day when possible.

Here are a few healthy options:

  • Red bell pepper: 95 mg per ½ cup raw
  • Orange juice: 93 mg per ¾ cup
  • Orange: 70 mg per medium fruit
  • Grapefruit juice: 70 mg per ¾ cup
  • Kiwi: 64 mg per medium fruit
  • Green bell pepper: 60 mg per ½ cup raw
  • Broccoli: 51 mg per ½ cup cooked
  • Strawberries: 49 mg per ½ cup sliced
  • Brussels sprouts: 48 mg per ½ cup cooked
  • Grapefruit: 39 mg per half medium fruit

Vitamin C also helps the body absorb iron, so it's smart to pair vitamin C-rich foods with iron-rich foods, such as chicken with red bell peppers or fish with Brussels sprouts.

Should I take vitamin C supplements during pregnancy?

It's probably not necessary to take a vitamin C supplement. But you might be wondering if it's safe, especially if you're hoping to boost your immunity duringcold and flu season.However, for most pregnant women, it's relatively easy to get the recommended amounts of vitamin C through a healthy diet and a goodprenatal vitamin.

"I don't use vitamin C supplements, but I make sure to eat a balanced diet full of fruits and vegetables," says Community mom Amber0608.

Research on taking vitamin C while pregnant is mixed, and the World Health Organization (WHO) does not recommend adding a separate vitamin C supplement to your prenatal vitamin. If you're concerned about your intake, ask your practitioner if a vitamin C supplement makes sense for you.

The bottom line? Eating ahealthy, well-balanced dietwith plenty of vitamin C-rich foods and taking a prenatal vitamin should more than cover your needs.
