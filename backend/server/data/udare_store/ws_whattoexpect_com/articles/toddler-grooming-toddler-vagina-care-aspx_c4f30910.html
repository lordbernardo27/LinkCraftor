When it comes to vaginal care, this special organ works rather well on its own. But occasionally things can get a little uncomfortable down there, even for a toddler. Just like fully grown ones, a child's vagina (the canal-like organ on the inside of the body) and vulva (the exterior part of the female genitalia) are susceptible to itchiness, irritation or even infection. How to clean your toddler girl's genitalia

Here's how you can ensure your toddler avoids genital irritation and infection:

  • Wipe the right way. At every diaper change, always wipe your toddler from front to back to avoid spreading bacteria that can cause an infection. (Give a play-by-play as you do this, too, so she'll know the drill when she's masteredtoilet-trainingand takes over this task.)
  • Check the creases. For poopy diapers, and especially if she has loose stools, clean the cracks and crevices around your child's vagina and labia. But there's no need to wipe away whitish discharge inside the labia since that's perfectly normal (though do call the pediatrician if this discharge suddenly changes in amount, color or odor as an infection may have cropped up).
  • Skip irritants. Choose unscented, alcohol-free soaps, body washes, bath oils, wipes, lotions and creams, which are less likely to irritate your toddler's tender skin. And avoid bubble baths altogether, which can irritate and even lead to aurinary tract infection, or UTI.
  • Save lather for last. Tub time is fun time, but sitting in dirty, soapy suds can be potentially irritating. The fix: Save soap and shampoo until the end of the bath. When it's time to wash your child's vulva, have her stand to rinse it with fresh water using a washcloth or cup to remove any residue and then whisk her out before she plops back down into the bubbly brine.
  • Keep the area dry. After diaper changes and bath time, pat your toddler dry before usingdiaper cream. If she gets wet from swimming or splashing outside, remove her wet items right away as too much moisture can irritate the sensitive skin around her vagina.
  • Choose cotton. When she's mastered potty training and starts wearing panties (yay!), choose breathable all-cotton ones. Other fabrics trap moisture and perspiration, which can irritate.

Signs and symptoms to watch for

It's not uncommon for toddler girls to develop a vaginal issue, so be on the lookout for the following symptoms of pain and discomfort that could signal irritation or an infection. White discharge

Yeast infections in toddler girls are rare (this condition is more of a teen thing), but yeast vulvitis is possible in tots who are still sporting diapers. If you notice white discharge, a yeast infection could be behind it. Treatment is often an anti-fungal cream or ointment. Foul smell

A stronger-than-usual odor from your little girl's urine could be the sign of a urinary tract infection, which is somewhat common at this age. An estimated 3 percent of girls will have a UTI by age 11.Antibiotics are the go-to treatmenthere, whether by mouth as a liquid or pill, or via IV in the hospital in more severe cases.

Pain

Soreness at the vaginal site is also a UTI symptom, as is pain or stinging while urinating. Harsh cleansers cause pain too, which is called soap or chemical vulvitis.

Vaginitis, a bacterial infection, also leads to pain and swelling around the vagina (or sometimes the vulva) and can stem from irritating detergent, wet diapers, swimsuits or germs. Soaking in a warm, soap-free tub can clean the area. Pat dry afterwards.

Redness and discharge

Petroleum jelly or diaper ointment can ease redness, though a topical ointment could also be prescribed. Avoiding soaps, fabric softeners and lotions can help as well.

Itchiness

Got scratching? Soapy bath water and bubble baths are the most common cause of genital itchiness in girls, though poor hygiene and less-than-thorough diaper changes can also contribute. Vaginitis and the itching that accompanies it may be due to leftover bits of poo or even a piece of tissue paper stuck in the vagina. Careful water-only cleaning and wearing loose clothes can ease the scratching.

Keep in mind that most irritation and infections in the vaginal area can often be quickly treated. Along the way, strive for thorough diapering as well as simple care down there when your toddler girl is in the bath, and then teach her good hygiene as you toilet train down the road.
