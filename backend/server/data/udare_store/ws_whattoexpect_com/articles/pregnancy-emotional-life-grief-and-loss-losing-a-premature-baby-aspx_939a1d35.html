How to Cope When You Lose a Premature Baby

Updates to copy, information, sources, and formatting

Losing a preemie can bring overwhelming grief, guilt, and questions. Here’s what to know about healing, honoring your baby, and finding support.

Losing apremature baby, either before or after birth, is devastating. Along with profound sadness, you may also feel guilt, anger, or confusion about not carrying the pregnancy to term or not being able to protect your premature baby after birth.

All of these emotions are common and completely normal after the loss of a preemie. It's important to remember that no one is to blame. In time, and with support, healing is possible.

Key Takeaways

  • Your grief is valid — and you are not to blame. Losing a premature baby can bring overwhelming sadness, guilt, anger, and confusion. These emotions are common and normal, and it’s important to remember that nothing you did or didn’t do caused your baby’s death.
  • Healing takes time, and support truly helps. Grief looks different for everyone, including your partner. Being patient with yourself, allowing space to mourn, and leaning on loved ones, counselors, or support groups can help you navigate this painful loss.
  • Honoring your baby can be a meaningful part of healing. Saying goodbye — whether by holding your baby, naming them, taking photos, or planning a memorial — can help you acknowledge your baby’s life and begin the long, personal journey toward healing.

Tips for coping with the loss of a premature baby

After losing a premature baby, you may find it helpful to keep the following suggestions in mind:

Be patient with yourself and your partner.

Allow yourself time to grieve and process what happened. Your partner may be struggling too, even if their pain looks different from yours. Losing a baby can strain your relationship, and you may benefit from seeing a grief counselor together.

“Initially I felt a strong urge to protect my spouse and considered shutting him out so I wouldn’t cause him further grief, but the opposite has been incredibly healing for us,” says What to Expect Community member JAA71224, who lost her baby at 20 weeks.

Keep the "what ifs" at bay.

It’s natural to replay events and question whether something could have been done differently. But the truth is that you did everything you could to carry your baby to term. Thepremature birth— and your baby’s death — was not your fault. Nothing you did or didn't do caused this outcome.

Reach out for the support you need.

Lean on trusted people in your life, such as family members, close friends, a therapist, or a spiritual leader. Ask your healthcare provider for a referral to a grief counselor, or look for a support group, either locally or online. You don’t have to go through this alone.

“I found a group for parents who have experienced infant loss through a hospital that meets monthly, and it is difficult to attend but makes you realize you are not alone on this absolutely horrible journey,” says What to Expect Community member mama1090.

Another member, Kiera-my-angel-baby, says, “I have good days and bad but have found support groups here (in the What to Expect Community) and on Facebook to be of enormous support.”

Find what comforts you.

People cope differently with grief, and it’s important that you give yourself space to find and indulge in healthful comfort.

“Journaling, support groups, therapy, walking, reading self-help books, writing notes to my baby, and holding out hope for the future are the only things that keep me going,” says What to Expect Community mom LBGirlMom, who lost her son at 21 weeks.

How to honor your premature baby

When a baby dies hours, days, or weeks after a preterm birth, the pain may seem like it's too much to bear. For some parents, finding a way to say goodbye can be an important part of healing. You may choose to:

  • Hold your baby in your arms
  • Take photos of your baby
  • Name your baby
  • Plan a memorial or burial, if that feels right to you

“My dad is making a baby name sign with her stats that we can put with her urn,” says What to Expect Community mom 406mommy.

There’s no right or wrong way to honor your baby — only what feels meaningful for you and your family.

“The first few weeks are so tender, so be gracious with yourself,” advises What to Expect Community member Nikki-15. “I miss my little girl every single day, but I have found ways to share her story and give her tiny life meaning.”

Resources to help you through the loss of a premature baby

In addition to support from family and friends, you may find comfort in connecting with others who understand what you’re going through. The following groups offer resources and support for parents who have lost a baby:

  • Share Pregnancy & Infant Loss Support: Offers local and online support groups, along with educational resources for grieving families.
  • Postpartum Support International: Provides support groups, specialist referrals, and other tools for coping with pregnancy and infant loss.
  • Star Legacy Foundation: Offers virtual support groups tailored to different types of pregnancy and infant loss.
  • Return to Zero: H.O.P.E.: Provides resources, professional referrals, and support for parents and families after the loss of a baby.
  • March of Dimes: Shares coping tips, community support, and educational resources for families navigating loss.

“While I can’t say it’s gotten ‘easier,’ I will say I can think of my son and have a few happy thoughts every now and then, instead of feeling crushed every time,” says What to Expect Community member irisv1998 about the loss of her premature son at 5 weeks.
