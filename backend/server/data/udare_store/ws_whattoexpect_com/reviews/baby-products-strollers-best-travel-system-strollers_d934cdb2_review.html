The Best Stroller Travel Systems That Parents Say Make Life Easier

best travel systems

Quick Summary

The best travel system is the Uppababy Vista V2 Stroller with the Mesa Infant Car Seat, according to parents who find it versatile, easy to use and maneuver, and great for convenient added features like spacious storage space and an included bassinet.

When it comes to traveling with a baby — whether it's a big road trip or just a few quick errands around town — I quickly learned, like all new parents, that convenience is everything. And nothing is more convenient than a travel system. These car seat-stroller combos make getting from point A to point B with your little one so much easier.

A travel system is basically afull-size strollerand a compatibleinfant car seatthat clips into the stroller when you're on foot or into the car when you're on (big) wheels. Car seat-stroller combos let you travel with your baby for years — I have been able to use mine from infancy right up through toddlerhood.

Most families need both a stroller and an infant car seat when they have a newborn. And in my experience as a mom of two, making sure the two products are compatible increases how much use you get out of both. Picture this: You’re driving with your baby in the backseat and need to stop to run a quick errand. Because you have a travel system stroller, you don’t have to unbuckle and lift your baby out of her car seat then re-buckle her into the stroller (possibly waking her up in the process). Instead, you simply unclip the entire car seat from its base and attach it to the stroller frame.

Not to mention, I’ve found that travel systems let you knock two big items off your baby checklist in one go and often allow you to save some money.

How We Chose the Best Travel Systems

To find the best travel systems, I compiled a list of around 50 car seat and stroller sets currently on the market. To whittle the list down, I referencedstroller safety recommendationsfrom from the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP): The group says strollers should have a five-point safety harness to keep your baby securely strapped in, easy-to-operate brakes and one footrest that extends across both sitting areas (for a side-by-side double stroller). I immediately eliminated any travel systems from the list with strollers that did not check all of these boxes.

I then considered car seats and strollers that I personally have tested and loved as a mom of two and parenting editor. I also noted the travel systems that parents in the What to Expect community have reviewed highly, and took into account feedback from parents on the What to Expect staff who have tested many of these strollers and car seats. Finally, I narrowed my list down to the best travel systems by showcasing those that have received top marks for:

  • Versatility
  • Maneuverability
  • Nice-to-have features (such as cup holders on the car seat, an adjustable stroller handlebar and washable fabrics)

FAQs

Should I get a car seat stroller combo?

This is a personal decision that really depends on your family’s needs. A travel system stroller allows you to switch your baby from car to stroller easily, but it might not be right for everyone.

A travel system can come in handy for families who are often out of the house. Because you already have the car seat installed, you only need to remember the stroller frame. A travel system can also help you save money: buying a car seat and stroller as a set may be cheaper than purchasing them separately. Still, there are some downsides to keep in mind before you invest:

  • The car seats that come in travel system strollers are for infants only, so you'll need to invest in a compatible toddler car seat later.
  • Even the lightest travel system stroller can be bulky, making it tough to load into and out of your trunk.
  • Infant stroller travel systems may not be good for dirt and gravel roads, since they're heavier than most strollers and tougher to push on uneven surfaces. They can also be tight squeezes through narrower grocery-store aisles.
  • You can only use the car seat that comes with the stroller (and not all companies sell extra car seat bases separately from the travel system). So if you have more than one car, you'll need to buy a second car seat if you don't want to unhook and reattach the one base the stroller comes with.

Is a car seat/stroller combo safe?

Car seat/stroller combos are very safe as long as they meet safety guidelines.

When buying any car seat (regardless of whether or not it’s part of a travel system), always make sure the product you’re considering:

  • Isn’t too old (you can check with the manufacturer or look for a label with the date it was made). Make sure, too, it has a label with the date of manufacture and model number.
  • Doesn’t have any visible cracks in it.
  • Comes with instructions so you can safely install the seat.
  • Isn’t missing any parts.
  • Wasn’t recalled (if you’re unsure, you can contact the NHTSA Vehicle Safety Hotline at 888/327-4236 or visit theirwebsite).
  • Hasn’t been in a car crash.

Strollers should have:

  • Easy-to-operate brakes that lock two wheels instead of just one to provide an extra measure of safety.
  • A wide base that can’t easily be tipped over.
  • A five-point harness with straps over both shoulders, hips and between the legs.
  • One footrest that extends footrest all the way across both sitting areas (for a side-by-side double stroller).

Once you’ve purchased a travel system, take these steps to ensure that your little one's secure:

  • As you put the system together, be sure to include all the pieces. There may be a lot of them, but every last one has a purpose.
  • Practice clipping the car seat into the stroller and unclipping it again before your baby's sitting in it (so you're a pro before you add a wee wiggler to the mix). Once your baby's in the car seat, gently shake the handle to make sure the seat's fully attached to the stroller before you hit the road.
  • Look for a label that reads "This restraint system conforms to all applicable federal motor vehicle safety standards. This restraint is certified for use in all motor vehicles and aircraft." This label means the seat has passed rigorous federal safety standards.

As always, remember that infant car seats should not be used for routine sleep, according to the AAP. The safest sleep position for a baby is flat on her back.

What are the different types of travel system strollers?

At their core, travel system strollers have a few things in common: They allow you to nestle baby’s car seat right inside and wheel away. But there are a few different types out there:

  • One-piece travel system strollers. These convert the car seat into the stroller versus having a separate stroller.
  • Two-piece travel system strollers. Most stroller systems are designed this way, with a separate car seat and stroller that work together.
  • Adjustable travel system strollers. These stroller systems allow you to change the height for your comfort — and baby’s.
  • Two-seat travel system strollers. While many travel stroller systems are designed for one car seat, some can fit two to accommodate twins.
