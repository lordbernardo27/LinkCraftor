Here's How Your Body Changes After Having Twins or Multiples

Light copy updates, updates to sourcing, and fact-checked for accuracy.

postpartum recovery from multiples pregnancy, mom with stretch marks holding newborn twin babies

Your postpartum recovery may take longer after having twins or multiples, but you will heal. Learn more about how multiples affect your body and how to manage.

Physically recovering from a multiple birth, whether it was vaginal or via C-section, is much like recovering from a singleton birth — only more so. Indeed, the postpartum period can be worse with twins or triplets, as everything you experience will feel just a little bit more painful and last a while longer.

"[After giving birth I] instantly felt lighter and relieved, even after just the first one was out!" says What to Expect Community user mckythe. "But I was caught off-guard by how much healing was necessary. I’d had two singletons where after delivery I felt absolutely fine, so I was especially surprised when I delivered the twins and was temporarily *less* mobile than I was before. Getting out of bed on my own was also just not an option the first couple days."

But know this: You’ll get through it and your body will go back to normal after twins or multiples (or very close to it!).

The best pathway for a successful post-birth recovery is to be patient and give yourself enough time to rest and heal. To help you understand what your body is going through, here’s more about how each part of you changes after giving birth to multiples, as well as whether you can speed up (or least ease) the postpartum recuperation period.

Key Takeaways

Recovery takes longer after multiples. Healing from a twin or multiple birth (vaginal or C-section) is similar to a singleton recovery but often more intense and prolonged, with extra soreness, bleeding, and fatigue.

Common postpartum symptoms include a stretched abdomen, cramping, sore breasts, heavier lochia, perineal or incision pain, backaches, and exhaustion — all of which gradually improve over several weeks.

You can’t rush recovery, but rest, hydration, nutritious food, gentle movement (once cleared), and postpartum care supplies can ease discomfort; contact your doctor if you experience heavy bleeding, fever, or other more serious symptoms.

How will my postpartum body look after having twins or multiples?

  • Stomach. Yourbaby bellyis starting to deflate, but don’t rush to the gym. Be patient and know that it’ll take longer for your midsection to return to normal after a multiple birth than it does after having just one baby. Your abdominal skin will be really stretched out after giving birth to twins and other multiples, to the point where it can be uncomfortable to walk and turn around. An abdominal binder can help provide support and relief. As for the pain, you’ll likely feel abdominal cramping for seven to 10 days as your uterus shrinks in size, with the strongest discomfort occurring around days two and three.
  • Breasts. Sore nipples andleaky, engorged breastsare to be expected when your milk comes in around three or four days postpartum. But once you get the hang ofnursing your twinsor multiples, if that's what you choose (or pumping if your babies are in the NICU), the discomfort should ease up. Be sure to eat plenty of healthy foods, drink lots of fluids, and get help from a lactation specialist if you need it. Not breastfeeding? Sit tight and know that your breasts often go back to their pre-pregnancy size as the baby weight comes off (though there sometimes are changes, so don't get disheartened if that's the case for you).
  • Vaginal bleeding. With twins and multiples, you’ll experience greater bloody postpartum vaginal discharge , which is called lochia. For the first 10 days this fluid will appear red and feel heavy, but then it’ll lighten in flow and color and end around the four- to six-week mark.   • Perineal pain. Thanks to pushing during labor or a possible episiotomy, you may have aswollen or sore perineum(that spot between your vulva and anus) that could last for a few weeks.
  • Back. Not only isback pain common in pregnancy, but it can very often continue after you give birth too.Postpartum back paincan stem from carrying those new babies all over the place, though pregnancy hormones also relax your muscles, ligaments, and joints, so it’ll take a few weeks before your back strength is closer to normal.
  • Body aches. You might feel sore all over from the birth experience, while your hips, knees, and feet that supported the baby weight (times two or more) will no doubt ache for several weeks postpartum.
  • Fatigue .Exhaustion is normal, since your babies' birth may have taken many hours to complete and, of course, you’ll be up all through the night breastfeeding or prepping and giving bottles to your sweetie pies.
  • Incision pain. If you had aC-sectionwith your twins, triplets, or other multiples, thestomach cutyou received may take up to six weeks to heal. Some numbing and itching at the site is also normal.

Is there any way to speed up your postpartum recovery after twins and multiples?

Unfortunately, there’s no surefire way to speed up yourpostpartum recovery, since the body simply needs time to heal. In general, if you delivered vaginally, you’ll likely feel better in three weeks, though it can be six weeks or longer if you had a tear or an episiotomy. A Cesarean recovery may take four to six weeks, though it’s not uncommon to feel some pain for months.

So while you can’t force your recovery to move swiftly along, you can gather supplies to ease your discomfort, such as a vaginal birth recovery kit that contains maternity pads, a peri bottle, and OTC pain relievers (though ask your doctor about what's safe for breastfeeding if you're nursing). And a stool softener may also be smart to have on hand in case your first postpartum bowel movement is slow to arrive, but again, check with your OB/GYN or midwife about laxatives and other medications before taking anything.

Keep up your energy as you recover, which includes a diet that’s full of complex carbs, fiber, healthy fats, and protein, along with plenty of water for hydration. And once youget the go-ahead to exercise, you’ll feel good about getting outside and moving your body. Just don’t rush theweight loss, as it can take at least six months and often longer to return to your pre-pregnancy size.

Remember, it’s completely normal to feel super tired and uncomfortable in the days and weeks after delivery, butcertain symptoms shouldn’t be ignored, such as very heavy vaginal bleeding, a bad headache, fever of 100.4 Fahrenheit or higher, or an incision that doesn’t seem to be healing. And you should call 911 right away if you feel chest pain or have trouble breathing.

Having two or more babies at once can do a number on your body, making the after effects somewhat worse and longer-lasting than if you’d had just one baby. Still, know that with enough time, plus help from friends and family, you’ll soon recover and be back to your old self again.
