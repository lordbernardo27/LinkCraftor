Best Products to Make Postpartum Sex Comfortable

Sex might be the last thing on your mind when you’re going throughpostpartum recovery. After all, your body needs time to recuperate after giving birth, whether you delivered via vaginal birth or C-section. Plus, it’s not just physical recovery you’ll need to navigate. Many women experience“the baby blues”for days and sometimes weeks after delivery, which can include anxiety, sadness and trouble sleeping and might not put you in the mood for intimacy. Additionally, sleep deprivation from caring for your new infant as well as being “touched out” from nursing or holding baby all day might also contribute to a decreased sex drive.

And once you are ready to have sex again, it’s possible that you’ll have a few issues to navigate, such as pain and tenderness. Fortunately, there are a number of techniques to help you find relief and products to makepostpartum sexas comfortable as possible. And, don’t forget that it’s okay if you still don’t feel like having sex even after getting the all-clear from your doctor. It’s important to take the time you need before getting intimate again, especially if you’re experiencing emotional or physical barriers.

Ahead, we’ll answer a few important questions about having sex after birth, such as how long to wait and what you can expect it to feel like. Then, we’ll get into the best products for postpartum sex, as recommended by experts and the What to Expect community.

When is it safe to have sex after giving birth?

Although there isno set timeframe, it’s typically recommended to wait four to six weeks after you’ve delivered before having sex again, according to theAmerican College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists(ACOG). Those who had anepisiotomyortear during birthmay be told to wait longer, until the area has completely healed. Women who gave birth via cesarean will also have a longer recoveryto give your incision time to heal.

You should get clearance from your practitioner before having sex after birth; consider broaching the topic during your firstpostpartum appointment, which usually occurs three weeks after delivery. And, even if you have the greenlight from your doctor, you should always wait until you feel ready and comfortable, as every person’s postpartum recovery looks a little different.

When you do decide to resume sex with your partner, go at your own pace when. “Just be careful when it’s the first time, go slowly, and do what feels right for you,” says Christine Greves, M.D., a board-certified OB-GYN at theWinnie Palmer Hospital for Women and Babiesin Orlando.

What does postpartum sex feel like?

When it comes to postpartum sex, the way it feels can vary from person to person. “For some women, it will feel totally normal,” saysJennifer Wider, M.D, women’s health expert. “For others, there may be some pain involved.”

Your pain factor may depend on what happened during your baby’s birth, especially if you had any tearing, says Dr. Greves. It’s common to feel vaginal soreness or pain after delivery, even if you had no tearing. For those who did tear, pain during sex might be more intense and longer-lasting. Breastfeeding may also impact postpartum sex: According to Dr. Greves, the decreased estrogen levels may lead to some vaginal dryness, which can cause more friction (and irritation) during sex forup to six and even 12 months after birth. If you’re experiencing soreness or dryness, you can try shifting your position during sex to relieve these symptoms. You might want to try being on top, on hands and knees or laying on your side to relax your pelvic floor, suggests Katie Ruebush, P.T., D.P.T. atMemorial Hospitalin Carthage, Illinois.

If, however, the vaginal pain doesn’t subside in two weeks or you’re concerned about the amount of pain you’re experiencing, you’ll want to reach out to your OB-GYN or physical therapist, recommends Ruebush. It’s possible you may be experiencing a type of postpartumpelvic floor dysfunctionin which muscles are too tight. This can result in painful ripping, tearing, or burning sensations during sex. If you and your OB-GYN expect this to be the case, you’ll want to reach out to apelvic floor therapistwho can diagnose you and provide a treatment plan.

Types of products to help with postpartum sex

Because there are several postpartum symptoms that can affect how you feel during sex, experts generally recommend having a few tools handy to keep you more comfortable while being intimate with your partner. They can include:

  • Lubricant or vaginal moisturizer: Both of these products help add moisture to your vagina, which can reduce friction and irritation. While lubricant is good to have on-hand for sex, you can use a vaginal moisturizer daily to help relieve dryness.
  • Perineal spray: Although you may receive a numbing spray likeDermoplastat the hospital, you might want to use an herbal perineal spray to soothe postpartum swelling and ease any discomfort without numbing the area. They usually contain ingredients like witch hazel and peppermint that can be cooling and soothing on any swelling.
  • Nipple cream: Nipple cream, especially one containing lanolin, can soothenipples that may be sore and crackedfrom breastfeeding, which can make rolling in bed and nipple play more comfortable.
  • A supportive and sexy nursing bra: You may be hesitant to go braless during postpartum sex. After all, your breasts have been through a lot of changes and may even be prone to spontaneous leaking if you breastfeed or pump. But a quality nursing bra can provide support to make you more comfortable. Not to mention they come in a number of sexy designs to further set the mood.
