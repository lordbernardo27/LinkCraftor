The Best Sippy Cups in 2025 include Olababy, EzPc, Philips, HoneyBear, Munchkin and More on colorful square background

QUICK SUMMARY

The best sippy cup is theMunchkin Miracle 360 Trainer Cup, according to testers from the What to Expect staff and user community. When choosing a sippy cup, experts and parents say the most important factors to consider are type, age range and ease of cleaning.

As a mom of two, I can tell you the journey frombottlestoopen cupsisn't always a smooth one. Both of my boys eventually made the switch, and sippy cups were an incredibly helpful stepping stone during that time. Not only are they easier for most babies and toddlers to drink from and hold independently, but they’re also typically less messy (weighted bottoms and covered tops prevent most puddles from appearing on your floor). Sippy cups are also more portable, allowing you to easily pack them in a bag for on-the-go activities like play dates and daycare.

But how do you know when is the right time to introduce your little one to a sippy cup? TheAmerican Academy of Pediatrics(AAP) now recommends starting to wean off bottles somewhere between 6 and 9 months. Understandably, there is quite a learning curve at this stage. While some experts recommend going straight to open containers from the breast or bottle, most families (mine included) found that the best sippy cups can helpbridge this gap.

How I Chose the Best Sippy Cups

To learn about must-have features and any safety considerations when using training cups before ensuring every recommendation was backed by both experience and expertise, I combined firsthand parenting know-how with insights from trusted professionals and organizations:

  • Interviewed pediatric and dental experts: I spoke to Lauren Crosby , M.D., a pediatrician at La Peer Pediatrics in Beverly Hills, California, and member of the What to Expect Medical Review Board; and Michelle Kelman , D.D.S., a pediatric dentist in Los Angeles, California, and a member of the What to Expect Medical Review Board.
  • Priotized parent-tested picks: As both a mom and former parenting editor, I have tested many sippy cups to find ones that work for my two sons, so I made note of the products that I’ve personally found most helpful and easiest to use. Next, I spoke to What to Expect staff members with four kids between them and then canvassed the What to Expect community boards to learn which sippy cups parents felt were easiest to clean, and for babies to hold and drink from.
  • Reviewed feedback from previous award results: For the2024What to Expect Feeding Awards, where hundreds of parents voted for their favorite baby products, sippy cups were included.

By blending expert guidance with parent reviews, this list spotlights the best sippy cups that are durable, easy to use, and (mostly) spill-proof — so you can focus more on your little one’s progress and less on mopping up messes.

A Doctor's Note on Sippy Cups

Dr. Kelman says she generally prefers that parents transition from a bottle directly to an open cup, but if you do go the sippy cup route, she suggests a spoutless model. “Although there have been some concerns regarding positioning of the tongue with [spoutless] cups, they aren’t as damaging to the teeth as traditional sippy cups,” she explains.

  • Comfort: This is particularly important when shopping for a sippy cup for a younger baby. The littlest hands may prefer cups made from materials that are soft, such as silicone, as well as those that have a softer spout that more closely resembles a bottle nipple.
  • Design: First, you’ll want to consider what type of sippy cup you’d like—hard spout, soft spout, straw, or flat lid. Cups that are designed to be easy to manage and hold, such as models with handles or grippy material, are often easier for little hands to pick up. It’s also smart to look for a sippy cup designed to prevent leaks: After all, one of the biggest benefits of using a sippy cup is that they help prevent spills as your child learns to drink from a cup. Some models on the market are truly leak-proof, while others are more what we'd call leak-resistant.
  • Ease of cleaning: Straws and spouts are a perfect place for gunk to build up and germs to grow, which means ease of cleaning is incredibly important. The best sippy cups are those that come completely apart and can be washed in the dishwasher to avoid the possibility of mold.

FAQs

What kind of sippy cup is best?

There are four main types of sippy cups: Hard spout, soft spout, straw and flat lid. We’ve featured each of the four types on our list of the best sippy cups, but it can be difficult to decide which is best for your child. Soft spouts and straws are popular choices, as they more closely resemble nipples and are easier for tots to learn how to drink from. “Straws are better for oral motor development, but most babies can't figure out how to use them before they are 9 to 12 months old,” says Dr. Crosby.

Besides straw and spout, many experts also recommend flat lid or spoutless cups. “I always suggest a spoutless one as the first option,” says Dr. Kelman.

Do pediatricians recommend sippy cups?

You don’t have to offer your child a sippy cup (you can go straight from the bottle to an open cup — and in fact, some experts recommend this), but the AAP says sippy cups are just fine to use while transitioning baby from the bottle to a “real” cup, which you should ideally do by about age 2. But they recommend that sippies are only used while your child is learning to make this jump.

At what age should babies start using sippy cups?

Although you don’t have to give baby a sippy cup, many parents do choose to offer one to babies in their first year, especially because an open cup can be difficult and messy for young children.

“We recommendintroducing a sippy cuparound 6 months old so the child becomes familiar with it and proficient so that by their first birthday they can use them and start weaning off bottles,” says Dr. Crosby.

Your child can begin using a sippy cup as soon as she can sit in a high chair to eat solid foods. “A cue that your child is ready for a cup is that they are sitting upright on their own,” says Dr. Kelman.

What cup should an 18-month-old use?

As your toddler approaches her second birthday, give her more opportunities to sip from an open cup or straw cup (such as when she’s sitting in her high chair eating). The AAP recommends that children be weaned from sippy cups by age 2.

A good rule of thumb? Try to think of the sippy cup as a bridge to the real thing, and switch to a real cup once she gets the hang of holding it upright, rather than letting it become a comfort object. “Toddlers don't need to carry around a cup all day like a security blanket,” says Dr. Crosby. “That tends to encourage over-drinking which can fill them up so they don't eat their meals.”
