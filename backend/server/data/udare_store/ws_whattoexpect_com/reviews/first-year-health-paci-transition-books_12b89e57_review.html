7 Sweet Stories to Help Your Little One Say Goodbye to the Pacifier (Including the Book That Finally Worked for My Toddler)

pacifier books

When my son was 20 months old, I helped him wean off the pacifier. Even saying those words is still a bit stressful. He first became interested in the pacifier around the 2-month mark, and that later turned into a full-blown obsession after weaning from breastfeeding.

To help our sanity, my husband and I purchased the book "Ben Gives Up His Pacifier" by Jenny Album. While there have been some ups and downs over the two months of weaning — like the full-on meltdowns at naptime — he finally stopped demanding his beloved paci.

If you have a paci devotee in your household, you know that there’s a window where you should really cut the habit, and this stage is daunting. "It's best to drop the pacifier between 12 and 18 months … because it can cause problems with speech and also with teeth and jaw development,” saysGina Posner, M.D., apediatrician at MemorialCare Orange Coast Medical Centerin Fountain Valley, California, and a member of theWhat to Expect Medical Review Board. “Babies typically get more addicted to using it the longer they have it, so 12 months is even better and actually tends to be easier.”

If you’ve committed to the dreaded transition, you know it’s easier said than done. In fact, "removing it suddenly can lead to resistance, tantrums, and even temporary sleep disruption (for everyone)," saysBrook Choulet, M.D., a child and adolescent psychiatrist and toddler mom.

“Books and even podcasts can help normalize how difficult the transition can be for both parents and their babies,” she says.

But which ones do you choose? Here are seven to add to your toddler’s bookshelf so you can finally bid farewell to the pacifier.

Goodbye, Paci!

Why We Love It

Is there anything Lovevery can’t do? The Montessori-based toy and play kit company sells a pacifier transition book that comes with a special bag for your baby’s pacis. It uses real images of a young girl taking on this difficult phase. She goes on a paci hunt, places them all in a bag, and feels a sense of accomplishment after one night without her soother.

You can also scan a QR code to access an interactive song about saying goodbye to your pacifier (genius!).

No More Pacifier, Duck

Why We Love It

Sometimes, it’s just more fun to follow a story about an animal. In this case, an adorable baby duck is getting too old for his paci, and Mama Duck first suggests only using it for bedtime. The book features plenty of repetition as Mama Duck says, “Only in bed, sleepyhead.” And one day, Mama Duck takes the plunge and says, “No more pacifier, duck. Not even in bed, sleepyhead.”

“This story uses simple, repetitive language, which is very helpful for a toddler to process concepts,” says Dr. Choulet. “It's a nice story that ends with a message of growth and pride, which makes toddlers feel good and positive about themselves.

Pacifiers Are Not Forever

Why We Love It

There are so many milestones your toddler has already overcome, and this book celebrates them all. With a focus on older tots who can’t give up their pacifier, it reminds them that they’re big enough to do things like walk, talk, and feed themselves — and now, it’s time to add tossing their pacifiers to the list. Parents will also appreciate that there are pages with step-by-step tips, like how to develop a strategy, plan the official goodbye, keep your little one busy, and comfort them when they need it most.

Ben Gives Up His Pacifier

Why We Love It

There were so many books to choose from, but the little guy on the cover reminded us of our own binky-loving baby. It follows Ben, a toddler who takes his paci everywhere — even in the bath (an all-too-relatable situation). The Pacifier Fairy visits Ben and shares that she needs his pacifiers for all sorts of things in Fairyland, gives some advice on coping without a paci, and promises to exchange them for special gifts. This is where parents need to come in and play Pacifier Fairy, so there is an extra step involved for you — but it's worth it. After two days, some tears, and a 40-pack of washable markers, I’m relieved (and honestly, shocked) to say my son is officially paci-free. For girl parents, author Jenny Album also wrote "Bea Gives Up Her Pacifier."

Pacita the Pacifier Fairy

Why We Love It

A pacifier fairy, appropriately named Pacita, is here to recognize the grief that comes with losing the pacifier, while also explaining how she’ll love and care for them. There’s a lot of rhyming in this poetic tale to encourage toddlers (and their parents) to approach this transition with confidence. Even more, there are four pages of bonus materials outlining advice for finding alternative soothing methods, staying positive, and penning letters from Pacita to your toddler.

Florrie the Paci Fairy

Why We Love It

Meet Florrie, another Paci Fairy with a superpower: She takes kids’ pacifiers and turns them into stars. There’s a sing-along component for music lovers, and the catchy tunes can even lull your little one into a restful, paci-less slumber (or keep them calm during a chaotic car ride). We love how one WTE parent decorated her daughter’s room with glow-in-the-dark stars, and another simply pointed to a real star in the sky to reinforce the book’s message.

Milo the Pacifier Hero

Why We Love It

There are fairies and then there’s 'Milo the Pacifier Hero.' Your kid’s new favorite superhero eases the transition by telling toddlers that he needs to give them to other babies in need. The rhyming story is fun and whimsical, and the customizable certificate is the ultimate reward your little one can hold onto. It teaches them about lending a hand — er, paci — to others and provides a physical token to celebrate their accomplishment.
