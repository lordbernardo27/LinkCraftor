Post-Sex Cramps During Pregnancy: Here’s What’s Typical — and What’s Not

Updates to copy, sources, and formatting.

hand squeezing orange, cramps and contractions after sex during pregnancy

If you're getting cramps and contractions after orgasm, there's probably nothing to worry about. Here's when it may be a sign of something more serious.

Was it too much to hope that cramps would end when you stopped having your period for nine months? Welcome to pregnancy and all the little aches and pains — and yes, cramps in your legs, back, and abdominal area — that come with it, including after sex.

Key Takeaways

  • Cramps after sex or orgasm can be normal. Mild cramping in your back, groin, or belly in the second or third trimester is common thanks to greater blood flow and a more sensitive cervix.
  • Gauge the timing and symptoms. Depending on how pregnant you are, cramps could be related to round ligament pain or Braxton Hicks contractions. But heavy bleeding, dizziness, pain, and frequent contractions are red flags (call the doc ASAP).
  • Take care of you. Be gentle with yourself and head to a warm bath or nap until you're feeling better. And if skipping sex for a while (or the big O) helps, give it a go.

Is cramping after orgasm or sex normal?

Yes, and in most cases it's par for the course. Sometimes post-sexcramps in your abs and groinarea come with spotting or a little extra blood flow, too. Neither of these symptoms should be remotely embarrassing, nor are they usually a reason to worry or give up on sex, but it's always a good idea to run them by your practitioner for reassurance.

Should you call the doctor — or not? Check out theWhat to Expect appfor expert advice about your symptoms, as well as comforting support from liked-minded mamas.

What causes cramping after sex?

Cramping after sex during pregnancy can have a few possible causes:

  • Cramping or painful twinges that may feel like contractions during or after orgasm in a normal, low-risk pregnancy are likely caused by increased blood flow to your abdominal area, as well as natural changes that make your cervix more sensitive.
  • Hormones in semen called prostaglandins can also bring on cramps, especially later on in pregnancy.
  • Menstrual-like cramps around conception can be caused byimplantation.
  • Rarely,cramps after sex may actually be labor contractions— especially if they're painful enough to make you double over or are accompanied by blood flow that won't stop. That could be a sign of something more serious, including early labor, miscarriage, or ectopic pregnancy. Give your practitioner a call right away.

You'll also want to talk to your doctor if your post-sex cramps come with symptoms likesevere dizzinessor headache to rule outpreeclampsia.

What should I do about cramping and contractions after sex?

After orgasm, your body sends a rush of blood to your uterus, which can cause those uncomfortable cramps in your groin. Don't force yourself to power through them.

Instead, give yourself a chance to recover with a little R&R until the discomfort passes. Some women also feel relief more quickly after a warm bath or a nap.

What to Expect Communityparent doodlesandy is in that boat: “I get contractions at orgasm and sometimes just during sex. It's annoying and distracting for both of us, but my husband knows to give me a minute when it happens. It happened with my other pregnancies too.”

If the cramps and contractions keep building and getting worse rather than subsiding, check in with your doctor right away to make sure your symptoms aren't a sign of something serious.

Is there anything I can do to prevent cramping and contractions after sex?

If orgasm leads to cramps and contractions that are too much to bear, skip the main event. You may find foreplay without the climax is just as much fun, especially if it doesn't leave you in recovery mode afterward.

If the prostaglandins in semen are contributing to the cramps, using the withdrawal method or a condom can also help prevent discomfort next time.

Are cramping or contractions after sex more common in early or late pregnancy?

Cramping and contractions can happen after sex at any time during your pregnancy, but they tend to feel more uncomfortable as your uterus expands in the second and third trimesters.

Cramping after sex in early pregnancy

Early on in your pregnancy, mild cramping after sex can be associated with implantation, which is the fertilized egg attaching to the wall of your uterus.

Severe one-sided cramping, withvaginal bleedingand dizziness, can be an indication of an ectopic pregnancy.

Cramping with heavy bleeding, a backache, and pelvic pressure that feels like the baby pressing down could potentially be a sign ofmiscarriageduring the first half of pregnancy.

Cramping after sex in late pregnancy

During the second and third trimesters, cramping after sex can feel more uncomfortable as your uterus swells and puts pressure on bones, muscles, and ligaments in your abdomen.

Cramping with lower belly pain, a symptom known asround ligament pain, can start in week 14 and get more severe as your pregnancy progresses.Braxton Hicks contractions, which are your body's way of preparing for birth during the third trimester, can make cramps after sex a double whammy.

Can cramping or contractions after sex induce labor?

As labor approaches, there's no avoiding the fact that cramps and contractions will become stronger. Your uterus is stretched to the max, your bones and ligaments are shifting around to make room for the baby's delivery, and it's likely that your body and mind are prepping for the big event.

If you're still enjoying sex, high five and keep it up — with your doctor's permission. It's unlikely that orgasm will actuallyinduce laborunless your body is ready, but with so many physical changes causing discomfort to your groin area, you might choose to dial back a little.

Just remember that cramps and mild contractions after sex and orgasm are usually nothing to be concerned about and not a reason to stop having sex.

But to put your mind at ease, talk to your doctor about it to get the reassurance you need that all is well with you and your baby.

When should I call the doctor about cramping after sex?

Cramps after sex are fairly normal at all stages of pregnancy, even if you never experienced cramps after sex before.

But occasionally they could indicate something is wrong. Call your doctor right away if you experience cramps combined with any of these other symptoms:

  • Fever or chills
  • Heavy spotting or bleeding, especially bleeding that doesn't subside
  • Painful headaches
  • Vision changes
  • Pain or burning during urination
  • Dizziness
  • More than four contractions in one hour and cramps that are painful enough to make you double over, as these may besigns of labor
