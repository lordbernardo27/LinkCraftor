October is here and the leaves are changing — and that means it'sfally’all. And one thing’s for sure, according to your October baby'sbirth flowers: This month will bring you a bright, calm child with a generous spirit.

What are the October birth flowers?

If you have an October baby, his birth flowers are the sunny marigold and the easy-going cosmos. You might associate this month more with pumpkin spice and flannel than flowers, but when it comes to predicting (or celebrating) your young one's personality, these October flowers offer some beautiful insight: a bright, well-rounded child, who draws others to him with his generous, calm nature.

Marigold: A symbol of the rising sun

Marigolds in shades of radiant orange, yellow and red are the perfect fall flower, though they’ve been blooming all summer long (and will keep blooming until the first frost). Cosmos: Balance in all things

Cosmos are a favorite among gardeners because they're easy to grow and their warm-colored blooms are a surefire way to attract every butterfly and pollinator in town. Native to Central America, cosmos are mainly scentless except for one dark red variety that smells like chocolate (nothing wrong there!). Incorporating marigolds and cosmos into your family's life

It's easy to add your October baby's fiery birth flowers throughout your home or greenspace. These fun ideas are sure to add some pops of color — and personality! — to your everyday life:

  • Gather in the garden. Marigolds and cosmos are pollinators' best friends, but the marigold can also be beneficial to add to your garden, too. Their aromatic foliage attracts insect pests away from other tender flowers and vegetables while repelling chipmunks and squirrels.
  • Build a boho bouquet. A bunch of fiery marigolds or lacy cosmos simply thrown in a vase make for great boho bouquets. It’s not just about the cheery, bright flowers but their intricate, lacy leaves. Want a fancier look? Check out the instructions for thiscosmos and summer flower arrangement.
  • Experiment in the kitchen. Both marigolds and cosmos are edible — marigolds taste peppery while cosmos taste like nectar. Try marigolds insaladsor as a garnish atop savory foods. Cosmos blooms make delightfulpastry decorations.Cosmos cupcakedecorating party, anyone?
  • Buy or DIY. Feltmarigoldsandcosmosmake great everlasting bouquets, or you can combine them with felt beads for a beautiful mobile that will definitely keep your little one occupied. You can either make your own or head over to your nearest felt flower or crafting shop, a laEtsy!
  • Try creating a rust nursery that isn’t rusty! The rusty marigold yellow/orange has made a comeback in nursery decor (from the 70’s). Check outthese nursery designsto fulfill all your decorating dreams.
  • Dye your heart out. Marigolds can be used to make a yellow-orange dye that is reminiscent of drops of sunshine. Dye onesies, bedsheets, curtains and more with thisnatural dye recipeor try out a marigold (or marigold-colored) tie-dye project together with your little one!
