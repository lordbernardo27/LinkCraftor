70 Baby Items You Didn't Know You Could Buy With FSA Money

FSA Eligible Baby Items

It’s no secret that having a baby is basically synonymous with spending money. Not only do babies require a lot of stuff, but so do pregnant and recovering postpartum moms. Fortunately, a Flexible Spending Account (FSA) can help you stock up on pregnancy and baby essentials, while saving money in the long run.

According to theU.S. Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, an FSA is a type of health plan that allows you to set aside pre-tax money to pay for certain out-of-pocket health care costs. And, with most plans, that includes purchasing health-related essentials as well as covering doctor’s visits. (AHealth Savings Account(HSA) is another health plan that works similarly to an FSA and can also be used on many of the items below.)

There is one caveat: Only specific baby, maternity and postpartum items are going to be eligible for purchase with FSA money. That said, there is a surprising amount of FSA-eligible baby items covered and include many that all new moms need to have on hand for a smooth recovery, as well as taking care of your newborn.

FAQs

Are diapers FSA-eligible?

Although diapers are a non-negotiable for babies, they’re unfortunately not FSA-eligible. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS)considersbaby diapers personal care items and not medical expenses, and FSA reimbursement is only available for medical expenses. Other baby items that are surprisingly excluded from FSA eligibility include infant formula and bottles.

How do I use FSA money on baby items?

You can typically access FSA dollars through insurance plans and company benefits. These accounts are a place to set aside pre-tax money and use it on qualified medical expenses. This includes things like prescription medicines and eye exams, but it can also cover certain baby, maternity and postpartum essentials that are determined medical expenses. The advantage of using pre-tax dollars to buy these items is that you end up saving money that you would otherwise be putting towards your federal and state taxes.

Your insurance policy will dictate whether you need to shop directly from the officialFSAstores, or if you can buy items directly from retailers like Amazon, Target or Walmart, or a pharmacy like CVS or Walgreens. Some insurances will give you a debit card to purchase these items, while others will require you to submit receipts. Either way, make sure that the item is explicitly stated as FSA eligible before purchasing.
