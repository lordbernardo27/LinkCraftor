9 Best Baby Beach Tents — Safe, Shady & Parent-Approved

Best Baby Beach Tents

QUICK SUMMARY

The best baby beach tent, based on expert's guidance and parents' feedback, is the Pacific Breeze Easy Setup Beach Tent because it's made of UPF 50 fabric, provides ventilation, is easy to assemble and spacious.

Pacific Breeze Easy Setup Beach Tent because it's made of UPF 50 fabric, provides ventilation, is easy to assemble and spacious.

As I prepared for my daughter's first beach trip three years ago, I made sure to have all the essentials:bathing suits,baby-safe sunscreens,baby sunglassesand a baby beach tent to keep her safe from the sun. Thought, I’ll be honest, I was hesitant about the latter. I like to pack light when I go to the beach, and the idea of lugging another bag wasn’t appealing. Plus, I figured my daughter would be fine with the typical sun gear. (Heck, I was a tent-free beach baby and I turned out fine.) But I was immediately proven wrong after talking to fellow moms and our pediatrician

"Sun protection needs to be multifaceted since — let's face it — we aren't perfect when it comes to applying sunscreen," saysHayley Goldbach, MD, FAAD, FACMS a double board certified dermatologist and dermatologic surgeon at Brown University. She recommends keeping your baby out of the sun during peak hours (from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.) when the UV rays and heat are the strongest. One way to do that is to utilize a baby beach tent, says Dr. Goldbach.

Baby beach tents, which can also be used at parks, playgrounds and your backyard, are made of sun protective fabric to protect your little one’s skin from the sun’s rays while giving them a comfy spot to stay cool — and even nap. But narrowing down the choices can be tricky, especially if you’re not used to using a beach tent for yourself. So to save you some time, I've done the research for you and found the best baby beach tents for your al fresco hangs.

How I Chose the Best Baby Beach Tents

Sun protection is, of course, the top priority when selecting a beach tent for your baby. But you also need to make sure that the tent allows for air flow to avoid overheating and just keep your baby comfortable. I spoke to Dr. Goldback and consulted recommendations from theAmerican Academy of PediatricsandSkin Care Foundationto learn what beach tent features keep your baby safe. Per their guidance, all beach tents on this list are made of UPF 50 fabric and feature mesh or roll-up windows to provide proper airflow and keep the space from getting too hot.( It’s worth noting that mesh windows won’t offer the same sun protection those made of the tent fabric and, therefore, should be placed away from the sun.)

With this information in mind, I then considered what I looked for when shopping for my first beach tent. I also spoke to a colleague who takes her son to the beach regularly, and turned to the What to Expect community to ensure my criteria met the needs of the masses. Based on this feedback, I made sure all of the baby beach tents on the list received positive reviews for:

  • Security : Some tents have sand pockets to help hold them in place while others have stakes. Ones with stakes can also be used at older sibs soccer games, backyard play dates or when you’re camping.
  • Portability : If you have a lot of children or tend to go to the beach without your partner, portability should be at the top of your list. Look for lightweight tents and one that comes with a carrying bag. Similarly, if you are going on a beach vacation, lightweight beach tents with carrying bags will be easier to pack.
  • Ease of use : Pop-up tents are the easiest to set up and take down. For others, you may want to check out a YouTube video before hitting the beach for the first time.
  • Durability : Sturdier tents are built to withstand strong wind gusts and will last more than a summer. They also tend to cost more, an investment that is worth it if your family spends a lot of time outdoors or you plan on having more children.

ThePacific Breeze classic pop-up tentwas the first baby beach tent I tried, and it truly checked all the boxes. And it doesn't hurt that it has thousands of 5-star ratings.

Made of a water-resistant, UPF 50+ polyester, this pop-up tent has three large windows and an open front to ensure plenty of ventilation. As much as I like to soak up the sun, I found myself escaping to the tent with my daughter when temperatures got a little too hot.

And that fact that it's not a total pain to carry or set up adds to the appeal. The tent is very lightweight — comparable to a camping chair, one user noted — and can be easily folded and stored in the included carrying case. As for set up, all you have to do is pull the two strings at the top of the tent and it'll pop into place. Just be sure you secure the tent with the four included sand stakes and sand pockets.

Another thing that drew me to the Pacific Breeze was its durability. “This is the third beach tent that I’ve purchased and the only one that has made it through multiple trips to the beach,” one parent says. I've had my tent for two years and though it's a little scuffed up, it still does its job.

When unfolded, the tent has enough room for four people, and features several pockets where you can store your phone, sunscreen or even a portable white noise machine.

So what's the downside? Unfortunately, there is no way to close the opening. So if you want complete privacy when breastfeeding or need to contain a super antsy baby, this may not be the tent for you.

Why We Love It

If your baby loves playing in the water but you’re worried about sun exposure, considerMonobeach’s pool tent. The tent comes as two separate parts: a dome covering and base that zip together to form a shaded area where your child can safely splish and splash. To utilize the pool feature, simply dig a not-too-deep hole, place the circular part of the base over it, and then fill with water.

The tent features a large roll-up back panel that offers plenty of ventilation to let in the breeze and keep your baby cool on hot days. There is also a small side pocket where you store toys and sunscreen. Included are four pegs that keep the beach tent secure from blowing away in the wind and a handy carrying bag.

As easy as this is to set up, many parents note that it is extremely difficult to fold and store. “Even with the included directions, it took two people and about 30 m[inutes] to figure it out,” one parent wrote. To save yourself some time and sanity, check out some YouTube videos and practice at home before hitting the road.
