Help Me, Heidi! What Salad Dressings Are Safe to Eat While I'm Pregnant?

"Hi, Heidi! What salad dressings are safe to eat while I’m pregnant? If the bottle lists egg yolks in the ingredients, does that mean it’s a no-go?"

Tired of hearing about all the foods and drinks that are tabled for two when you’re pregnant? Here’s some good news from the expectant eating front, for a change. You won’t have to take a pregnant pass on most salad dressings, especially commercially prepared ones (including, most likely, the one in the bottle you were scanning).

Key Takeaways

Most salad dressings are safe in pregnancy. Commercially prepared dressings — including Caesar and blue cheese — are typically made with pasteurized eggs and dairy, eliminating the risk of bacteria like salmonella or listeria.

Be cautious with homemade or restaurant versions. Always confirm whether eggs and cheeses are pasteurized before eating; if unsure, opt for vinaigrettes or dressings made with commercial mayo.

Postpartum, all dressings are back on the menu. Once you’ve delivered (and even while breastfeeding), it’s safe to enjoy any type of dressing — including those made with raw eggs or unpasteurized cheese.

The risk you’ve probably heard about is from salad dressings (and other sauces, like hollandaise, béarnaise, or homemade mayo) that are made with raw eggs. The pasteurization processkills off bacteria (like salmonella)that raw eggs can harbor — which means they can’t bug you or your baby. So go ahead, seize that bottle of commercially prepared Caesar and get busy. Just make sure you follow the label instructions about refrigeration.

What about restaurant-prepared dressings? Some kitchens use pasteurized eggs in their recipes, others don’t — so ask, and if you’re still uncertain, take that pass after all. Vinaigrettes are a safe bet, as are creamy dressings made without eggs (say, a ranch made with commercially prepared mayo). Feeling blue cheese (or roquefort), but worried that the soft cheese it contains might becontaminated with listeria? Once again, you can stop stressing and start shaking, since commercially produced prepared dressings are almost always made with pasteurized cheese (and it will likely be specified on the list of ingredients). In restaurants, check in before digging into blue cheese dressing, or those tempting crumbles. Chances are they’re made with pasteurized dairy, but confirmation will allow you to rest (and eat) better.

Making your own? Just use pasteurized eggs (or commercially prepared mayonnaise) and pasteurized soft cheese to blend up the dressing of your dreams. A trick I use at home: Substitute mayo for those raw eggs plus oil to make your Caesar praise-worthy but pregnancy-safe. (Not that I’m pregnant at the moment, but it’s really that good — especially with extra parmesan and extra anchovies.)

Other ideas: "I use Herbs de Provence, white vinegar, and a splash of olive oil," says What to Expect Community user potatertot. "The possibilities are endless if you have the staples. Whenever I have a Greek salad I'll do the same but with Greek seasoning."

Taking a peek ahead to your postpartum salad days, and all dressings will be back on the menu — even that table-side Caesar made with raw eggs (that is, once you’re able to score your first meal out). And, yes, that's the case even if you’re breastfeeding your little sprout (raw sprouts will be back on the menu, too, in case you’re interested!).
