The 9 Best Baby Floor Seats, According to Parents and Experts

best baby floor seats

Baby floor seats can be a godsend for both you and your child. Parents like these seats because they provide a safe place to keep your little one secured and occupied when you need your hands free, whether you’re folding laundry or starting dinner. A baby seat can also give your little one a chance to sit up and have an exciting new vantage point from which to view the world before they’re able to sit up on their own.

These floor seats, which are best suited for babies 3 to 5 months old who are able to control their heads, can help your little one learn how to sit up, a very important milestone.

How We Chose the Best Baby Floor Seats

  • We spoke withMeghan Hunter, DO, a pediatrician withPhoenix Children’s Pediatrics, for guidance on how to safely use a baby floor seat as well as what features to look for when shopping for a floor seat. Per her guidance, each floor seat this list is supportive of baby’s neck, back and thighs and includes a safety harness.
  • We spoke to four members of the What to Expect editorial team who have six kids between them and have tried several of the floor seats included here.

What to consider when buying a baby floor seat

Baby floor seats come in different styles, be made of different material, and feature different add-ons like activity centers or snack trays. Here are some factors to consider as you start your research.

Fit: Babies come in all shapes and sizes, and even if they fall within the weight and height guidelines for a certain seat, they may not necessarily fit comfortably in it. For instance, one common complaint of floor seats is that the leg holes aren’t large enough for chubby thighs. If you can't try the seat in person to see how it fits your baby, order from a site with an easy return policy just in case it isn’t comfortable for baby. "The best and most development-friendly baby floor seats will provide enough support for your baby without being too restrictive," says Dr. Hunter. In short, you’ll want a seat that baby can’t fall or wriggle out of, but also one that isn’t too constricting.

Ease of cleaning: Floor seats get gunky quickly, so consider how you're going to degrime it. Plastic and foam are simple to wipe down, and if your seat has fabric panels, they should be easy to detach and machine wash. This is especially important if your floor seat doubles as a booster chair and you're planning to use the seat at mealtimes.

Interactiveness: "Look for seats with developmentally-appropriate toys to help stimulate your baby’s development," says Dr. Hunter. This may include an activity center with buttons, knobs and rattles to encourage fine motor skill development, or a musical bar that will introduce them to new sounds. Your baby floor seat may also include a removable tray. "This is convenient for feeding or playtime and provides additional support to prevent the baby from toppling forward," says Dr. Hunter.

Age-appropriateness: Some seats are meant for young babies only, while others can convert into booster seats and be used well into the preschool and toddler years. These seats can be good investments because you can use them for many years, but might not be necessary if you already have a high chair or other feeding gear.

Portability: You’re likely planning on using a floor seat around the house, whether you need to sit baby on the floor while you fold clothes or in the kitchen as you cook. Because of this, it may be ideal to prioritize seats that are lightweight and easy to move around without hassle. Some are designed to fold easily while others are made of lightweight material like foam.

Are baby floor seats safe?

Baby floor seats are safe when used correctly. Here's what you need to know about how to safely use a baby floor seat.

Keep it on the floor: Sometimes reviewers' online photos show baby floor seats placed on tables or counters, but this is a serious no-no. “As the name implies, baby floor seats should only be used on the floor, not higher surfaces such as a table, bed or counter,” says Dr. Hunter. “No matter how safe the seat looks, there is still a chance that the baby can topple over.” The exception: Some floor seats come with straps that attach to high-backed chairs and can do double-duty as boosters. Under no circumstances, however, should these be used on a backless chair or stool.

Wait until your baby has neck strength: Make sure your baby has proper head and neck control before setting them up in their new spot. "It is very important to not use floor seats until your baby can hold their head up on their own,” says Dr. Hunter, whichusually happens between three and five months.

Set up safely: "Floor seats should be kept out of range of dangerous objects such as electrical sockets, cords, and sharp objects," says Dr. Hunter. Find an open space where your baby won't be tempted to grab anything that could harm them.

Limit the time: Dr. Hunter recommends only allowing your baby to use the floor seat for a total of 30 minutes each day, broken up into 15-minute increments. "When babies are confined to a 'container' such as a baby floor seat, they lose some freedom of movement,” says Dr. Hunter. In fact, this containment can be restricting, especially to their spine and hips. It can potentially impact their anatomical development and cause delay in gross motor skills, she adds.

Encourage movement: No matter how cool your new chair looks, or what the company claims, it isn't going to teach your child how to sit — the muscles needed for sitting must be developed simply by using them. "You want to make sure your baby has plenty of free time away from their seat to learn to use their muscles and gain strength," says Dr. Hunter.

Supervise: You'd be surprised by the trouble babies can get into even when you just duck out for a second to answer the doorbell. "Parents should never leave their baby unattended in a floor seat," says Dr. Hunter. Floor seats should not be used as a replacement for supervision and should always be used while you’re with baby.

With these safety tips and buying guidelines in mind, check out the best baby floor seats below.
