The 10 Best Stroller Footmuffs To Keep Your Baby Warm on the Coldest Winter Walks

Best Stroller Footmuffs

QUICK SUMMARY

The best stroller footmuff is the JJ Cole Bundle Me Bunting Bag. It wins points from parents for being compatible with all strollers, as well as its adjustable design and removable hood.

You probably know to stock up onbaby mittens,snowsuits, and warm hats before your little one’s first winter. But one item you might not have on your radar is a stroller footmuff.

Also called stroller buntings, footmuffs attach to your stroller to keep baby warm and dry during cold-weather walks. They look like little sleeping bags, cocooning baby in insulated, water- or wind-resistant materials for added comfort.

A stroller footmuff might not be a must-have for every parent, but they can come in handy depending on where you live — especially in colder climates where most days are plagued by freezing temps, snow, or rain. And, unlike a blanket, footmuffs aren’t easily kicked off by a wiggly passenger.

As a mom of two living in Brooklyn, New York, I relied on mine for four winters. While not exactly an essential product, I absolutely found my footmuff much easier to use and more secure than wrapping my babies in multiple blankets during walks.

A Doctor's Note on Footmuffs

“If you live in a city and are walking around with your child in a stroller, a footmuff can be really helpful, especially for babies who are not as good at regulating temperature,” saysMicah Resnick, M.D., a board-certified pediatrician in Cincinnati, Ohio, with TriHealth and member of the What to Expect Medical Review Board. Of course, you can still bundle up baby in layers, he notes, but a footmuff provides “a great level of convenience and ease.”

Just note that any footmuffs designed for car seats should never include a layer of fabric between baby and the car seat or harness straps, and their face should always be uncovered in the car, per theAmerican Academy of Pediatrics (AAP). Any added bulkiness is unsafe because it can impede the way the harness works. “If using a footmuff with a car seat that you put in the stroller, remember to ALWAYS remove the footmuff before putting the car seat back in the car, as the fabric behind baby is not safe to be in the car seat when operating the vehicle,” saysTina Feeley, M.D., M.P.H., a board-certified pediatrician practicing at Chestnut Hill Pediatrics in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts and member of theWhat to Expect Medical Review Board.

How I Chose the Best Stroller Footmuffs

When putting together a list of the best stroller footmuffs for babies, I started by comparing feedback on stroller footmuffs from What to Expect community and staff members. Referencing their reviews and recommendations, I compiled a list of around two dozen stroller footmuffs that received praise for their quality, compatibility, and ease of use.

Next, I interviewed three pediatricians (Dr. Resnick;Gina Posner, M.D., a board-certified pediatrician practicing at MemorialCare Medical Group in Fountain Valley, California; and Dr. Feeley, for additional information about stroller footmuffs, including how to use them safely. I also referenced information from theAmerican Academy of Pediatrics(AAP). And since I’m a mom of two and a former parenting editor who has tested several stroller footmuffs myself, I took my own experience into consideration, too.

Based on all of this research, I narrowed down our list of the best stroller footmuffs to the top 10 that checked the following boxes:

  • Made from comfortable, weather-resistant materials
  • Easy to use and adjust
  • Has anti-slip features to prevent bunching

I also awarded “bonus points” for any compelling additional features, such as extra insulation (for footmuffs for babies in very cold climates), universal compatibility, and machine-washable constructions (they tend to get pretty dirty after walks in the snow or slush).

What to Look for in Stroller Footmuffs

To make sure you are going to get the right stroller footmuff for your family, these are some important aspects to keep in mind when shopping for a footmuff:

  • Material: Just like when you're shopping for a coat, the climate you live in will most likely inform what type of material to look for in a footmuff. For instance, if you live in colder climates, you might want to look for bunting that's insulated with multiple layers of protection. However, if your area doesn't get too cold, opt for a more lightweight option to prevent overheating.
  • Size: Most footmuffs are designed either for newborns to 6 months or 6 months to 4 years old. When in doubt, look to the sizing guide for each product to make sure it will fit your child. “Ideally, a footmuff comes up to baby’s armpits, so the face should always not be obstructed,” saysTina Feeley, M.D., M.P.H., a board-certified pediatrician practicing at Chestnut Hill Pediatrics in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts and member of theWhat to Expect Medical Review Board.
  • Adjustability: There are a few features that can make it easier to adjust your footmuff. Some, for example, prioritize designs that include sections that you can unzip to extend the length of the bag to fit baby as she grows. A few others on our list also provide front panels that can fold down to allow more air in if it isn't too cold out or fold up to keep baby more bundled. Your footmuff may also have an adjustable harness to ensure it's secured to the stroller.

FAQs

Are footmuffs safe?

Footmuffs are perfectly safe to use as long as they're the appropriate size for your child and properly fit your stroller. If the footmuff doesn't fit the stroller or baby correctly, the fabric could potentially cover baby's mouth as a result of bunching or the footmuff moving and pose a suffocation risk. That’s why Dr. Posner recommends footmuffs with anti-slip features to ensure your little one can’t move in their harness.

It’s also important to monitor your little one forsigns of overheating, adds Dr. Resnick. “If your baby feels warm to the touch, is sweating, if their skin is red and clammy, or if they are breathing fast, unzip the footmuff.”

How do I determine if a footmuff is compatible with my stroller?

The easiest way to ensure that a footmuff you’re considering works with your stroller is to buy one provided by the same brand. (For example, if you have a Nuna stroller, you’d purchase the brand’s compatible footmuff.) But that shouldn’t rule out other universal footmuffs. For these, check the product description to see if the manufacturer lists specific brands or styles it’s compatible with. Or, prioritize adjustable features that work on a variety of strollers, such as adjustable straps and zippers, a removable back panel or expandable fabrics.
