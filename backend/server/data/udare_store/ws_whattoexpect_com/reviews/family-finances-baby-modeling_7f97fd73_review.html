Baby Modeling

How to get your baby or toddler into modeling, plus what experts recommend parents think about before putting their baby in front of the camera.

If your social media feeds have become a montage of your baby’s best moments since birth (or perhaps your baby even has her own account!), maybe you’ve wondered if your little one is meant for something bigger than a few here-and-there likes.

It’s only normal. Baby modeling has become more of a reality, thanks to the rise of social media. Many celebs (think: Brooke Shields, Natalie Portman, even Anderson Cooper) even did their share of baby modeling before making it big.

But experts say the business of putting your baby in front of the camera is about more than smiles and a little bit of cash. Here's what to consider if you’re thinking about having your child try her hand at baby modeling, plus the best way to get started.

Key Takeaways

Getting started in baby modeling involves finding a reputable agency (avoid upfront fees), submitting recent photos, and setting up necessary legal paperwork like a work permit and bank account for your child.

How to get your baby or toddler into modeling

As a parent, you play a big role in every decision of your child’s modeling career. The most important first step? Find a legitimate baby modeling agency. Whether you’re submitting photos or looking for an agent, you want to make sure you’re working with a reputable company.

To find a trustworthy agency, first, do your research (look up different company websites, make phone calls, set up in-person appointments) and avoid any large, upfront fees — they could signal a scam. According to experts, trustworthy agencies will take an agreed percentage from your baby's earnings as payment.

Other things to expect:

  • Plenty of paperwork. A good agent and agency will tell you exactly which forms you need to complete.
  • You'll have to keep your baby's photos up to date. When submitting photos, you'll want to make sure that you're using recent ones. Many agencies recommend photos of your baby in simple clothing on a white background.
  • You'll need a bank account for your child. You’ll also likely need to set up a bank account for your baby and get working papers as well as doctor's clearance. Many states, for example, require children have a work permit and a blocked trust account for the money they earn. (You can look up the specific child entertainment laws in your state at the U.S. Department of Labor's website .) How much do baby models earn?

Experts say $25 to $75 an hour is a typical range for a child model. However, keep in mind that rates can vary tremendously depending on the project and a brand's budget.

Tips before getting your baby into modeling

Still think baby modeling might be a good idea for your little one? Here are a few more considerations before getting started, according to experts:

Know your child’s temperament

Spend a little bit of time on any modeling agency’s site and you’ll notice that most do look for a particular “something” in babies — namely a child who interacts with the camera and who works well with adults, other kids, and new faces in a smiley, calm fashion.

In other words, if your infant is sociable, enjoys being around new people and reacts well to cameras, she may enjoy the photo shoot experience. Trying out a practice photo shoot to see how your child reacts can help you gain insight into whether modeling is right for her.

Adjust your expectations

It’s easy to assume that professional photo shoots might happen a lot like the ones in your living room: a few takes, lots of laughs, cute snapshots in the end. But that’s not always the case, and as a parent, it may help to go into the experience knowing that you may need to adjust your expectations.

For one, experts point out that modeling can require hours of waiting and uncertainty, often for little compensation. This can be stressful for both kids and parents, especially if you’re not expecting it.

“When I lived in New York City, I did submit my son for castings because they had them all the time,” says What to Expect Community user KClee. “He really doesn’t have the disposition for it though. They have to be ‘on’ almost all the time, and it can be a very long day.”

Some parents whose children have modeled say that scheduling can also be a big challenge since you may not always get much advance notice about opportunities. “[A photo shoot] was way more involved than I actually expected and a very long day for [my baby], especially since he was still at three naps a day,” says What to Expect Community user janicedpt.

To help streamline the process (and ease stress) it might help to have one person — a parent, grandparent or caregiver — be the point person for upcoming shoots.

Put baby's safety first

Most importantly, you'll want to make sure that your little one's health and safety needs are met.
