Hot Days, Cool Mocktails, and a Baby Bump: What Summer Pregnancies Really Feel Like

Updates to copy, sources, and formatting.

a pregnant woman standing on the beach in a bikini

Maxi dresses and pool days? Great! Morning sickness and ultra-hot temperatures? Not so great.

Ah, summer: backyard barbeques, beach vacations, fresh lemonade, and ... a baby the size of a watermelon sitting on your bladder all day? While this time of year can invoke a breezy sense of freedom, being pregnant in the summer (especially if you're in the third trimester) can also be challenging, to say the least.

Pregnancy comes with a kaleidoscope of symptoms, and the heat can make many of them extra difficult. But there are also several reasons to love summer pregnancies.

That flowy, comfortable maxidresshiding in the back of your closet? You get an all-access pass to wear it around-the-clock. On your way to doctor appointments? No need to invest in amaternity winter coator step gingerly on icy sidewalks. Making sure to stay active and hydrated? Say “yes, please” to a dip in the pool and a refreshingsummer mocktail.

And remember, you’re far from alone. Below, moms in the What to Expect Community reveal the good, the bad, and the totally relatable things that anyone who’s experienced a summer pregnancy will understand.

Key Takeaways

  • Heat can be a doozy. The higher the temp, the worse some pregnancy symptoms may feel, especially in the third trimester (think sweating and swelling …)
  • Summer clothes rock right now. There’s no need for layers — hurray! You can kick back in comfort and style with flowy maxi dresses and bathing suits.
  • Savor the flavor. Yup, we’re talking endless runs for milkshakes and cones as you stay hydrated and cool by the pool. Having a summer pregnancy? Lucky mama!

Summer pregnancies: The cons

"My doctor told me toavoid saunas and hot tubs. I laughed and asked if my car counted. I'm thinking I may just live in my pool, under an umbrella." — Minty_tea

"As a first-time-mom (and even now) people would always say, 'Oh, it's going to be a long, hot summer for you!' Or, 'That's terrible you'll be pregnant in the dead of summer!' Not so nice of them to give you something else to dread." — Lawn1214

“I had my daughter at the end of August during a summer that was insanely out of the ordinary hot. I was so puffy and swollen and sweaty ... just yuck." — winterbaby316

“My first was born [in September], and it was miserable! I had swollen feet for the last four months, I was hot ALL the time and you are constantly sweating. Winter pregnancy has been much easier and seems to help limit some of the end-of-pregnancy symptoms.” — cdcmommy

"Being huge and prego and SWOLLEN caused by heat and sun is not fun ... No shoes fit, even flip-flops were tight. Wedding rings are also out.” — Country_Momma26

Summer pregnancies: The pros

“Bring on the heat and sunshine! Not to mention pregnancy pool floats! Good for the pool, good for napping on your tummy!” — SAG34

“Flip-flops! It’s already getting hard to put on socks and shoes." — Goombas_mom

"Water fun [is the best]. You will soon find (if you haven't already) that being in a pool or the ocean and being buoyant is the most comfortable, weightless solution for all your aches and pains and [is] just plain relaxation. You can do this any time of year, but it is more readily available in summer." — Lawn1214

“[Being pregnant in the summer] saves space in the hospital bag [by] not having to pack winter clothing.” — cristalma21K

“This is my second August baby. I have a season pass to the water park so I can live in the lazy river being a human submarine! Ice cream is a must!” — Good-A-Neff

“I’m delighted to be pregnant in the summer again. I loved it last time! Summermaternity clothesare so much nicer than winter ones, easy to get summer shoes on and it’s not cold when you get up in the night to use the bathroom." — anon290718

What to Expect Community parent emmarrap loved her summer pregnancy wardrobe. “I was in a bikini or a sun dress 95% of the time and it didn’t matter if something didn’t fit because it was so hot I could have as much skin out as I wanted.”

“For all the teachers, [it’s great] spending the last two months on vacation and then starting maternity leave just as the school year is starting!” — VTbabyMJW

“My first was a summer pregnancy, and yes to the maxi dresses and flip-flops! Even better, sitting in the shade on a warm day with my swollen feet in the kiddie pool. All while eating frozen Thin Mints and homemade slushies." — Tandabat
