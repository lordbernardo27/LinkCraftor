The Blueberry Trick: Simple Exercises to Prep Your Pelvic Floor for Birth

Updates to copy, information, sources, and formatting

pelvic floor pregnancy

Your pelvic floor muscles work hard during pregnancy and childbirth. Learn how to take care of them with simple exercises.

Like many women, you may not have given your pelvic floor muscles much thought. But pregnancy and childbirth give these crucial muscles an incredible workout, and taking care of them — with exercises to both strengthen and relax — can prevent problems during pregnancy, birth, and postpartum.

It doesn't take intensive workouts, either. Read on to learn more about the pelvic floor, how you can care for it while pregnant, and simple exercises to try.

Key Takeaways

  • Your pelvic floor works hard during pregnancy. These muscles support your organs, help control bladder and bowel function, and stabilize your core — and they’re put under increasing strain as your baby grows and during childbirth.
  • Both strengthening and relaxing the pelvic floor matter. Exercises like Kegels can build strength early in pregnancy, while learning to consciously relax those muscles later on can help prepare your body for labor and delivery.
  • Proactive care can prevent issues later. Regular pelvic floor exercises — and guidance from a provider or pelvic floor physical therapist if needed — may reduce the risk of postpartum problems like incontinence or prolapse.

What is the pelvic floor?

Yourpelvic flooris made up of several layers of muscles and connective tissue that sit at the base of your pelvis. This system plays a key role in many essential functions, including supporting your pelvic organs, controlling bladder and bowel function, regulating abdominal pressure, and stabilizing your spine and core. The pelvic floor is even involved insexual functionand orgasm.

Although you might not think of it often (if at all), your pelvic floor deserves attention during pregnancy. While anyone can experience pelvic floor issues, they're especially common during and after pregnancy.

How does pregnancy affect the pelvic floor?

During pregnancy, your pelvic floor muscles must support more weight as your baby grows. What's the purpose of pelvic floor exercises?

Many pelvic floor experts recommend preparing your pelvic floor for pregnancy and childbirth with both strengthening and relaxation exercises. Research suggests that learning how to both engage and release your pelvic floor can go a long way towards helping prevent post-pregnancy pelvic floor dysfunction. You may be familiar withKegels, but these exercises are just one way to care for your pelvic floor during pregnancy (andpostpartum, too). In fact, many women don’t realize how important it is to also know how to relax these muscles (more on that below).

"I had physical therapy after my first and it was learning how to relax my pelvic floor that changed everything for me!" saysWhat to Expect Communitymom RYT1735.

Which exercises strengthen the pelvic floor?

In early pregnancy and through thesecond trimester, most experts recommend focusing on building strength and endurance. This helps your body adapt to the increasing demands on your pelvic floor as your baby grows.

To do this, practice pelvic floor contractions (aka Kegels) regularly. You can work them into yourexercise routine. For example, if youlift weights, try engaging your pelvic floor as you exhale during a bicep curl.

You can also add pelvic floor work to your warm-up or cool-down — for instance, by performing 10 to 20 contractions before and after awalkor bike ride.

Make sure you're performing a full pelvic floor contraction and release each time. Try this:

  • Sit comfortably with your knees bent.
  • Take a deep inhale, feeling your ribs expand.
  • On the exhale, gently contract and lift your pelvic floor.
  • Imagine that your pelvic floor is an elevator that you’re trying to slowly move from the bottom floor of a building up to a higher floor. Or imagine picking up a blueberry with your vaginal muscles. These visualizations can help ensure you're engaging the full muscle group.

Which exercises relax the pelvic floor in preparation for labor?

As you enter thethird trimester, it becomes increasingly important to focus on relaxation. Your pelvic floor needs to fully release and stretch during labor to allow your little one to pass through!

"I think being able to connect with those muscles and actively release them between pushes helped me to not get too tired and not get a bad prolapse," says Community mom MrsMamaBell.

It may feel unfamiliar to practice relaxing and lengthening the pelvic floor. But this skill can come in handy during thesecond stage of laborwhen you're pushing. Visualization can help:

  • Begin by sitting comfortably and taking a few deep breaths, feeling your ribs expand.
  • As you inhale, picture a flower gently opening.
  • Try to feel your pelvic floor soften and release as you do this. Repeat for 10 to 20 breaths.

You can work this into an exercise routine or practice anytime you think of it.

When should I talk to my doctor about pelvic floor concerns?

Some women experience symptoms of pelvic floor dysfunction during pregnancy or postpartum, includingpelvic organ prolapseor bladder issues such asleaking urine. Talk with your doctor ormidwifeif you notice any unusual symptoms at any point.
