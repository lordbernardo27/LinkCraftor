Best Products for Pregnancy Hemorrhoids

best products for pregnancy hemorrhoids

Your first trimester symptoms have come and gone — good riddance, morning sickness! — but once you’re in your second or third trimester, there are new pregnancy symptoms on the horizon likeankle and foot swellingand evencongestion. Another unfortunate symptom that might appear around the second trimester?Pregnancy hemorrhoids.

Hemorrhoids are varicose veins in or around the rectum that can be itchy and painful, especially while sitting or pooping. During pregnancy, these swollen, twisted veins areusually caused by poor blood flowand increased pressure within your lower pelvis, saysAimee Eyvazzadeh, M.D., a board-certified OB-GYN. And it’s no surprise that you might experiencepostpartum hemorrhoids, too.

“This pressure could be due to the growing fetus, pushing during labor or differences in bowel movements [during pregnancy],” Dr. Eyvazzadeh says. “Increased progesterone levels can cause slowing of the gut and less frequent bowel movements or constipation, which could cause more hemorrhoids.”

Thankfully, we’re here to help with advice and vetted over-the-counter hemorrhoid medications to help relieve discomfort and pain. And, if you’re also experiencing postpartum hemorrhoids from delivery, don’t worry — you can certainly use these products after baby is born, too.

How Do You Treat Hemorrhoids During Pregnancy?

Modifying your diet and habitscan significantly reduce hemorrhoidsduring and after pregnancy. But for hemorrhoids you experience during pregnancy, Dr. Eyvazzadeh says you may want to consider the following:

  • Try to have regular bowel movements that are not too hard; taking stool softeners could help
  • Drink a lot of non-caffeinated fluids
  • Get regular exercise, especially cardio activities like walking or swimming, which can increase blood flow and help prevent hemorrhoids
  • Take measuresto prevent constipation, like eating a high-fiber diet
  • Avoid sitting for long stretches of time by moving around to take the pressure off the veins in your pelvic area
  • Using witch hazel pads can helprelieve pain and reduce swelling
  • Soak in a warm bath

Do Pregnancy Hemorrhoids Go Away?

Typically, yes, hemorrhoids during pregnancy tend to go away after delivery. But don’t think that it’s as instant as “baby out, hemorrhoids gone,” because hemorrhoidscan still happen withinone month postpartum.

“Medication commonly given during labor can cause constipation and hemorrhoids,” says Dr. Eyvazzadeh. She advises patients to start taking a stool softener a few weeks prior to their due date to try and avoid constipation and unnecessary straining before delivery, but you’ll want to ask your doctor if that’s right for you.

Although hemorrhoids aren’t typically a cause for concern,hemorrhoids that worsendespite lifestyle changes and over-the-counter treatments may mean it’s time to talk to your doctor. Possible surgical treatment after pregnancy may be discussed. And if younotice any bleeding, speak with your doctor. Even though it could be a normal symptom of hemorrhoids or an anal fissure, it’s better to be on the safe side.
