Quick Summary

According to parent testers,Mustela’s foaming shampoois the easiest to use and clears cases of cradle cap fast.

The first time I noticed crusty yellow scales covering my newborn daughter’s otherwise perfectly adorable scalp, my heart did a flip. I immediately dialed my pediatrician’s office to figure out what was wrong. Thankfully, the diagnosis was pretty straightforward: it was a case of cradle cap. Totally harmless, totally painless, and totally treatable with one of the best cradle cap shampoos.

Also known asseborrheic dermatitis, cradle cap is basically baby dandruff. It usually appears in the first few weeks of their life as red, flaky patches on the scalp. (It can also be found in baby skin folds and in the diaper area, although that’s less common.) While it looks uncomfortably dry and itchy, it isn’t. In fact, the worst part might be how much it bothers you.

Luckily, localized cases of cradle cap are simple to treat. New parents may be afraid of washing their baby’s scalp if cradle cap presents, but don’t be frightened: TheAmerican Academy of Pediatrics(AAP) advises washing your baby’s hair every other day to gently remove and loosen up new flakes. That means you’ll need to have a great cradle cap shampoo on hand. Below, you can check out a few parent and pediatrician favorites.

A Doctor's Note on Cradle Cap

“Cradle cap is a typical newborn scalp condition that is very common and involves a mix of oil production, dead skin cells, and yeast building up on the newborn scalp,” explainsGabriella Dauer, M.D., FAAP, pediatrician atDauer Pediatricsin South Florida.

Most babies will experience mild or moderate cases of cradle cap; it’s rarely serious. “In more severe cases, a crusty mass may cover most of the scalp and even extend onto the face, neck, and behind the ears,” addsDina Elbeltagy, M.D., dermatologist and medical writer. If that’s what you’re seeing, you’ll definitely want to check in with your pediatrician.

Shampoos FAQs

How often should I wash my baby’s scalp and hair with shampoo, especially if they have cradle cap?

You don’t need to wash your baby’s hair every day, even if they have cradle cap. Dr. Dauer recommends washing their hair twice weekly at a very young age. “They’re not really getting dirty or out playing to accumulate dirt and sweat at that very young age,” she says. “Your doctor can help determine the frequency with which to use a medicated shampoo with ketoconazole, but shampoos with pyrithione are safe to use daily.”

“While there isn’t a higher limit, I often recommend parents to freely wash their child’s scalp as often as required, especially with a mild baby shampoo. Every second or third day is ideal and plain water works just as fine if washing the scalp daily,” echoes Dr. Pezad.

What triggers cradle cap?

“No one knows for sure the exact cause of cradle cap,” says Dr. Pezad. “There are several theories that suggest possible etiologies such as mother's hormonal changes during pregnancy which stimulate the infant's oil glands.” Experts believe that sebum (the body’s natural oils) and yeast (which lives on your skin) play a role as well, but it’s unclear exactly how they interact to cause seborrheic dermatitis aka cradle cap.

How likely is cradle cap to come back after treatment?

Even if you’ve managed to get rid of cradle cap completely with shampoo, it can still come back. “Since cradle cap is a form of dandruff, it can come and go for a while, even after treatment,” Dr. Elbeltagy says. She recommends regular gentle shampooing and brushing to prevent it from recurring. If it’s very persistent, you should speak with your pediatrician.

What’s the best remedy for cradle cap?

Contrary to many parents’ first instincts, washing a cradle cap-afflicted scalp is one of the most effective ways to address the issue. “Wash the scalp every second or third day with a mildbaby shampoo,” advises Dr. Pezad. “Thereafter use a soft toothed comb to brush off the accumulated scales in severe cases.” Massage baby’s scalp gently with your fingers, a brush or comb while shampooing to help loosen the scales. Put baby or mineral oil on the scalp before bathing to help with super stubborn scales. In more severe cases, your doctor might prescribe a cortisone lotion to use after bathing to help reduce inflammation.
