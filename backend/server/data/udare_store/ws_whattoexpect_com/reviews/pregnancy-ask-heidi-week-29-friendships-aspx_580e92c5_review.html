Why Pregnancy Changes Your Friendships — and How to Stay Connected

by Heidi Murkoff , author of What to Expect When You're Expecting.

Updates to copy and formatting.

"My friends don't have babies, and they're already treating me differently. I'm afraid things won't be the same with them after the baby's born."

Things aren’t the same already — as you’ve already noticed. Your friends want to check out the latest spring collection, you’d rather shop for a layette. They’re trying on bikinis, you’re trying on snuglis. They’re ordering bottles of beer at the bar, you’re ordering baby bottles online. They’re watching the latest chick flick, you’re watching the latest childbirth video.

What to Expect Community member Doreana knows this tricky divide all too well. “Ever since I became a mom with my firstborn, my friendships have changed so much — some for the better and some, unfortunately, not so much,” she shared. “It hurts when you feel like some of your close friends aren’t interested in your child.”

The truth is, friendships do evolve over the course of your life, with so much depending on whether you and your friends are traveling on the same life path at the same time. But those changes don’t have to spell the end of relationships you value. Maintaining friendships takes work, but it’s usually well worth the effort.

Key Takeaways

  • It’s normal for friendships to change during pregnancy. As your life shifts, some relationships may feel different — but that doesn’t mean they’re over.
  • Talking it out can really help. Letting your friends know you miss them can bring you closer.
  • Two-way conversations are crucial. Asking about your friends’ lives — from big developments to everyday details — will help them feel seen and valued.

How to navigate friendship speed bumps

Here are some tips for getting your friendships back on track — by meeting your friends halfway:

  • Talk about it. A simple "I miss our time together" can be all it takes to get your friendships back on course. Let your friends know that you treasure their friendships and that you’d like them to continue.
  • Expand your horizons. Tune in to your words the next time you’re with your friends. If you find yourself complaining 24/7 about your nausea or talking a baby blue streak ("You wouldn’t believe how many times he kicked today!"), try changing up the conversation. Your friends may miss the old you — the one who wasn’t obsessed with finding the perfect stroller. Explore topics of conversation that you all can relate to — the latest bestseller or movie, for example — and your connection will likely feel strong again.
  • Don’t forget that they have lives too. Yes, you’re the one who’s pregnant, but you’re not the only one with a life worth talking about. Your friends will be more responsive to what’s important in your life now (baby, baby, baby) if you’re responsive to what’s important in theirs (a promotion, a roommate problem, a breakup).
  • Have patience — and ask for it too. Your friends may just need some time to get used to the idea of you becoming a mother. Let’s face it: It’s a big adjustment for all. Be sensitive: Some friends may even be envious of your pregnancy. Give it a few months and they may come around to the new you (plus baby). And ask them to indulge you just a little as you learn to adjust to your new life.

Curious about all the changes of pregnancy? TheWhat to Expect apphelps you track your symptoms and stay on top of each stage.

Luckily, most friendships — especially the strong ones — can endure even the most trying of life changes. So hang in there. Besides, who knows — maybe you’ll start a trend among your friends. And before you know it, you’ll be talking about the best stroller — and they’ll be taking notes!

To good friends,

Heidi Murkoff
